# Changelog

All notable changes to osrlib are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). The package version is the public API promise; `schema_version`, the integer stamped into saves, commands, and events, is the separate serialization axis defined by [the specification](docs/spec.md).

## [Unreleased]

## [2.3.0] - 2026-10-31

### Added

- Each party member now has a place on a battle's range track, so members can stand at different distances from the monsters (#209). [`BattleState.advance_feet`][osrlib.crawl.battle.BattleState.advance_feet] gives each member's place in feet forward of the party line, where the party stood when the encounter opened, and [`BattleState.arrived_round`][osrlib.crawl.battle.BattleState.arrived_round] gives the round they got there. A member's distance to a group is the group's `distance_feet` minus the member's advance. Every member starts a battle on the line, at zero. Both fields default to empty, so a 2.2.0 save loads with every member on the line, and [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4. The [adaptations register](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/adaptations/#the-battle-round) gives every rule.
- A member can close on a group and attack it in the same round (#209). Declare `action="attack"` with `move="close"` in the member's [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration]. The member closes in the party's movement step and attacks in the missile or the melee step. The session judges the attack where the close will leave the member, and judges a melee attack against the front rank that every declared move produces. A member reaches a group in melee when their distance to it is at most their encounter rate plus 5 feet. So a thief can close and back-stab in the party's free round, from up to 35 feet away in leather armour under the default `basic` encumbrance.
- The new function [`preview_battle_round`][osrlib.crawl.battle.preview_battle_round] judges the declarations planned so far for a round without playing it (#209). Call it after each choice on a battle screen. It returns a [`BattleRoundPreview`][osrlib.crawl.battle.BattleRoundPreview] with each declaration's rejections, each member's projected advance, and each group's projected front rank. It runs the same checks as [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound], leaves out the roster check, takes no draw, and changes nothing.
- Two new functions in [`osrlib.crawl.battle`][osrlib.crawl.battle] report positions for a policy of your own or for server-side code (#209). [`member_distance_feet`][osrlib.crawl.battle.member_distance_feet] returns a member's distance to a group, and [`group_front_rank`][osrlib.crawl.battle.group_front_rank] returns the members who can fight a group hand to hand this round without moving.
- The player view shows a battle screen everything it needs to draw the positions and offer each member's choices (#209). Each new field has a default, so a client that ignores it keeps working.
    - [`EncounterView.advance_feet`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.advance_feet] gives each living member's place on the track.
    - [`EncounterGroupView.member_distance_feet`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterGroupView.member_distance_feet] gives each living member's distance to the group.
    - [`EncounterGroupView.front_rank`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterGroupView.front_rank] lists the members who can attack the group in melee this round without moving, and whom the group's melee reaches. It's the same value as `group_front_rank`.
    - [`EncounterView.formation_width`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.formation_width] gives how many members can fight side by side where the party stands, or `None` when `formation_width_limit` is off.
    - [`EncounterView.party_free_round`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.party_free_round] is true in the party's free round after it surprised the monsters, the round in which a back-stab is legal.
    - [`MemberView.encounter_rate_feet`][osrlib.crawl.views.MemberView.encounter_rate_feet] gives how far a member can move in a round, after encumbrance and *haste*.
    - [`EncounterGroupView.lit`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterGroupView.lit] says whether a light a member carries reaches the group, and [`EncounterGroupView.seen_by`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterGroupView.seen_by] lists the members who can see it. The session refuses nothing because of light or sight, so use these fields to decide what your game offers and how it describes the fight.
- The new event [`MemberMovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MemberMovedEvent] (`battle.member.moved`) reports each member who moves by themselves, closing on a group or falling back alone (#209). Its fields are `character_id`, `move`, `advance_feet`, `group_id`, and `distance_feet`. It reports the member's place right after the move, before the party line moves back, so use it to narrate the move and read the player view after the round to draw where everyone stands.
- [`DeclarationFizzledEvent`][osrlib.core.events.DeclarationFizzledEvent] has a new code, `battle.melee.fizzled`, and a new optional field, `group_id` (#209). The session reports it when a declared melee attack can't resolve when the party's melee step comes, for example when the monsters win initiative and run before the member's close. Its `reason` is `combat.attack.out_of_reach` or `battle.declaration.not_in_front_rank`, so tell the player why the member didn't swing. The field defaults to `None`.
    - The reason is `battle.declaration.no_target` when the group has no living monster the member can see left to strike, for example because the party's missiles or spells killed it earlier in the round. The session checks this before it checks where the member stands, so the member gets it whether or not they closed. A slow attack gets the same check at the end of the round.
    - The reason is `combat.attack.out_of_reach` when the group ran out of the battle before the attack came up, even for a member who stood within 5 feet of it when the round was accepted.
- Two new refusal codes for a move set on an action that can't take one (#209). [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] refuses a `cast` that sets any `move` with `battle.declaration.cast_cannot_move`, because a caster can't move in the round they cast. It refuses a `turn_undead`, a `use_item`, or a `hold` that sets any `move`, and an `attack` with a `fighting_withdrawal` or a `retreat`, with `battle.declaration.move_not_combinable`. Each has the member's id as its `character` param. An attack that closes, by a member who can't move, gets the existing `battle.declaration.cannot_move`.

### Changed

- A `close` now moves only the member who declares it, at that member's encounter rate (#209). Before, the first `close` in a round moved the whole party at its slowest member's rate. To bring the whole party up, send a `close` for each member. A member stops 5 feet from the group, and stops short of 5 feet from any other group still in the fight. A member already within 5 feet doesn't move.
- A fighting withdrawal or a retreat that not every declarer makes now moves that member back on the range track, at half their encounter rate or their full rate (#209). Any member can make one, wherever they stand, and it needs no replacement. Once the member has moved, the members then nearest each group form its front rank. When every declarer makes the same one, the whole party still moves as one body, and members who stand apart stay apart.
    - [`BattleDeclaration.replacement_id`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.replacement_id] now only trades places in the marching order, and the trade moves nobody. Marching order decides who stands in front among members at the same distance from a group. To bring a member farther back into the front rank, declare a `close` for them.
    - The codes `battle.declaration.no_replacement` and `battle.declaration.replacement_too_far` are gone, and a withdrawal no longer gets `battle.declaration.not_in_front_rank`. If your game handled them, remove those cases.
    - `battle.declaration.replacement_unavailable` now means the named member isn't another declarer, is falling back too, or was named by a withdrawing member earlier in marching order.
- The ranks toward each group now come from distance (#209). They're the living members sorted by their distance to the group, then by the round they got there, then by marching order, and cut into rows of the formation width. A melee attack needs the member within 5 feet of the group and in its front rank. Before, the ranks were the marching order whatever the distance. [`EncounterView.front_rank`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.front_rank] is now the first row toward the nearest group, which is the old value until members stand apart. To decide who can attack a group in melee, read that group's `front_rank` instead.
- Monsters now close on the nearest member they can see and strike only what they reach (#209). A group stops as soon as it's within 5 feet of that member, so it never walks past a member it can see. An invisible member doesn't stop it, and a member turned to stone does. A monster's melee attack resolves only on a member in the group's front rank within 5 feet of it, so the members in front screen the members behind. A `melee` from a policy of your own that aims at anyone else is skipped.
    - Monsters now close and attack in the same round. The default policies follow a `close` that brings a member within reach with a `melee`, so a group that closes strikes a round sooner than in 2.2.0.
    - An NPC adventurer with a missile weapon or a spell with one target picks from the first row of the party's ranks toward its group, at any distance. A breath that hits one target, like the hellhound's, picks the same way.
    - A breath, or an NPC's area spell, covers the rows toward its group from the front, by each row's distance. Under `aoe_friendly_fire`, a party member's area spell catches the members of the target group's front rank who stand within 5 feet of it.
    - A petrifying gaze reaches the members of the gazer's group's front rank within 5 feet of it.
- Distances in a battle are now measured from each member (#209). A missile's range band and a spell's range are measured from the member's place. A group that runs leaves the battle once it stands more than 120 feet from every living member. After a formation retreat, the pursuit starts from the standing member nearest a group that can follow. A ring of protection with a 5-foot radius protects the members in the wearer's row toward the attacking group, the members at the wearer's distance from it.
- Some command logs recorded under 2.2.0 replay differently under this release. That's the replay contract: a replay is valid only under the engine version that recorded it. To continue a 2.2.0 game, load its save with [`load_game`][osrlib.persistence.load_game], which restores the saved state and runs no commands. To reproduce a 2.2.0 game exactly from its log, install `osrlib==2.2.0` in a separate environment and replay the log there. To catch a log from another version before it replays, pass the save's `engine_version` to [`replay_game`][osrlib.persistence.replay_game] as `recorded_engine_version`.
    - The replay raises [`ContentValidationError`][osrlib.errors.ContentValidationError] when the log contains a round this release refuses, like a `cast`, `turn_undead`, `use_item`, or `hold` that sets a `move`, or a melee attack by a member whom a 2.2.0 `close` had carried into reach with the rest of the party.
    - The replay plays out differently when the log contains a `close`, an `attack` that set `move="close"`, which 2.2.0 ignored, a fighting withdrawal or a retreat that one member made alone, or a monster group that closed on the party. A battle plays as before when no member moves and no group closes.
- [`RemoveMember`][osrlib.crawl.commands.RemoveMember] without `give_to` now refuses to remove a member who carries an item the adventure depends on, so the party can't lose a quest item by dropping the member who held it. The adventure depends on an item when a gate on a door or transition, a trigger that can still fire, or any clause of a quest that isn't completed names its catalog id. The session refuses with `party.remove.carries_quest_item`, and the rejection's `character` and `item` params give the member and the item. Name a member in `give_to` and the item moves to them, so the removal goes through.

### Fixed

- An attack that ignores the defender's shield no longer raises `AttributeError` when the shield is magic. The bug was present in 2.2.0. Before, an attack on a retreating member who carried a magic shield, or an attack from behind on such a member, crashed the round. Now the attack is made against the armour class the defender would have without the shield, so a magic shield loses its enchantment along with the +1 every shield gives. A cursed shield that makes armour class worse still counts, because the rule takes away a shield's bonus and not its penalty. [`AttackContext.behind_target`][osrlib.core.combat.AttackContext.behind_target] and [`AttackContext.defender_retreating`][osrlib.core.combat.AttackContext.defender_retreating] describe the rule.

## [2.2.0] - 2026-10-30

### Added

- The new referee command [`RemoveMember`][osrlib.crawl.commands.RemoveMember] takes a character out of a running party for good. Use it when a character leaves play, like one who died too long ago for *raise dead*, and send [`AddMember`][osrlib.crawl.commands.AddMember] to bring in a replacement. The command is legal in `town` and `exploring`, makes no draws, takes no time, and reports a player-visible [`MemberRemovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MemberRemovedEvent]. Name another member in `give_to` to hand them everything the character carries. Without it, the character's gear and coins leave the game, and the party's experience for treasure doesn't change. The session never gives the removed id to another member, and it refuses to remove the last member who is neither dead nor turned to stone, so add the replacement first.
- A thief can back-stab in battle. Set the new field [`BattleDeclaration.back_stab`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.back_stab] to `True` on an `attack` declaration. osrlib accepts it only for a member whose class has the `back_stab` ability, like the thief, and only for a melee attack. The monsters also have to be surprised: the session accepts a back-stab only in the free round the party gets when it surprises the monsters, while [`BattleState.monsters_hold_rounds`][osrlib.crawl.battle.BattleState.monsters_hold_rounds] is above zero. A back-stab after a hide in shadows or move silently roll isn't available yet (#208). An accepted back-stab gets +4 to hit, ignores the defender's shield, and doubles its damage. The new field [`AttackRolledEvent.back_stab`][osrlib.core.events.AttackRolledEvent.back_stab] is `True` on each of its attack rolls, so your game can say so. When the session refuses a back-stab, it uses one of four new codes: `battle.declaration.back_stab_not_attack`, `battle.declaration.back_stab_no_ability`, `battle.declaration.back_stab_target_aware`, and `battle.declaration.back_stab_not_melee`. Both fields default to `False`, so a saved command or event without them still parses, and a declaration without `back_stab` plays exactly as before.

## [2.1.0] - 2026-10-30

### Added

- [`SpawnNpcParty`][osrlib.crawl.commands.SpawnNpcParty] has a new `count_fixed` field for an exact band size, like a band of four adventurers (#167). Before, the size had to be a dice expression, and `"4"` isn't one. Set at most one of `count_fixed` and `count_dice`. With neither, the session rolls the B/X number for the band's kind, as before. A saved command without the field still parses.
- The new referee command [`EndEncounter`][osrlib.crawl.commands.EndEncounter] ends an encounter peacefully, when the party pays a toll, talks its way past, or walks away (#167). A referee command is one your game issues itself, not on a player's behalf. `EndEncounter` closes the encounter, and the battle if one is open. The session logs the command, so a saved game and a replay close the encounter too. Before, the only way was to call [`end_encounter`][osrlib.crawl.encounter.end_encounter] directly, and a replay of that game stayed in the encounter.
    - Set `outcome` to `"parley"` when the party talks or pays its way past, or to `"departed"` when the party walks away from monsters that let it go.
    - Set `resolve_area` to `True` to mark the keyed area the monsters came from as resolved, so they aren't there the next time the party walks in. A keyed area is a room or cave that an [`AreaSpec`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.AreaSpec] defines, with its monsters and treasure.
    - The command moves no coins. To charge a toll, issue a [`RemoveCoins`][osrlib.crawl.commands.RemoveCoins] beside it.
    - An adventure can use the command as a [trigger's consequence](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/guides/gates-triggers-quests/#scripting-the-dungeon-with-triggers) too.
- Two new referee commands take items and coins away from a member (#173). Use them to run a shop that buys back armour, charge a price or a toll, or use up a quest item. The session logs them, so a replay repeats them.
    - [`RemoveItem`][osrlib.crawl.commands.RemoveItem] takes units of a mundane item, or one magic item or valuable (a gem or a piece of jewellery) by its instance id. An equipped item comes off first, and any effect it attached ends. A revealed curse doesn't stop the removal.
    - [`RemoveCoins`][osrlib.crawl.commands.RemoveCoins] takes exactly the denominations the command lists, and makes no change from other coins.
    - Both are legal in every mode, cost no draws and no time, and report an [`ItemsRemovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.ItemsRemovedEvent] with the code `session.items.removed`. A draw is one random number the session takes from one of its named [random streams](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/reference/rng-streams/).
    - When the member in `character_id` doesn't have the item or the coins, the session refuses the command with `items.remove.not_carried` or `items.coins.insufficient`.
- The new referee command [`AddMember`][osrlib.crawl.commands.AddMember] adds a character to a running party, like a replacement for a character who died or an adventurer the party meets (#172). Build the character as you build a starting member, and send it in the command. Then read the new member's id from the event the session reports, and use it in later commands.
    - The command is legal in `town` and `exploring`. In every other mode, the session refuses it with `session.command.wrong_mode`.
    - The session adds a copy of the character at the back of the marching order and gives it the next `character-NNNN` id. It reports the new member in a player-visible [`MemberAddedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MemberAddedEvent] with the code `session.member.added`.
    - The character must be new to this game. The session refuses a character who already has an id with `party.add.id_assigned`, one who has a condition or a stat modifier with `party.add.under_effects`, and one who carries a magic item or a valuable with `party.add.carries_instances`.
    - The new member keeps their mundane gear and coins. The coins a new member brings into a dungeon earn the party no experience.
    - The party's experience for a delve, one trip from town into a dungeon and back, is shared evenly among the members who survive it. So a member who joins during a delve gets an equal share of everything the party earns on it, including what it earned before they joined.
    - The command makes no draws and takes no time. The session logs it, so a saved game and a replay have the new member too.
- [`GrantItem`][osrlib.crawl.commands.GrantItem] can grant a magic item by its catalog id, so a game can sell potions or hand out a magic reward (#173). Each copy arrives unidentified, with a separate instance id in the [`ItemAcquiredEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.ItemAcquiredEvent]. The session rolls whatever the item's entry in the OSE SRD leaves to chance, like a wand's charges, on the treasure stream ([`TREASURE_STREAM`][osrlib.core.treasure.TREASURE_STREAM]), as it does for a magic item found in a cache. A mundane grant works as before.
- An adventure document can use `RemoveItem`, `RemoveCoins`, and a `GrantItem` of a magic item as trigger consequences and quest rewards (#173).
    - A `RemoveItem` can name its member with [`CARRIER_SELECTOR`][osrlib.crawl.triggers.CARRIER_SELECTOR] (`"@carrier"`). The [interpreter](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/guides/listeners-and-flags/#the-interpreter) replaces it with the first living member in marching order who carries the item. When nobody does, the interpreter drops the command with a note. A quest reward written this way uses up the quest item when the quest completes.
    - [`validate_adventure`][osrlib.crawl.adventure.validate_adventure] checks the item id of an authored `GrantItem` or `RemoveItem` against the equipment and magic item catalogs. It accepts `"@carrier"` only on a `RemoveItem`.
- The [`Rest`][osrlib.crawl.commands.Rest] command has a new `hidden` field, and [`GameSession.advance_turns`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.advance_turns] has a matching `hidden` parameter. Set `hidden` when the party rests quietly in an out-of-the-way place, which the OSE SRD says lowers the chance of wandering monsters. Each wandering-monster check during a hidden rest lowers the chance by 1, for example from 2 in 6 to 1 in 6, after noise and bright light raise it. On a level with the usual 1 in 6 chance, the session rolls no check during a hidden rest. In town no checks run, so `hidden` changes nothing there. The field defaults to `False`, so a saved command without it still parses as a plain rest. This also changes a plain rest: it no longer gets the lower chance. If your game wants the odds a rest had in 2.0.1, pass `hidden=True`. The Changed entry "A plain `Rest` in a dungeon is now riskier" gives the details.
- [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration] has a new field [`replacement_id`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.replacement_id], so a member can make a fighting withdrawal or a retreat alone (#171). To withdraw one member, send a `BattleDeclaration` for that member with `action="move"`, `move="fighting_withdrawal"` or `move="retreat"`, and `replacement_id` set to the back-rank member who steps up into the front rank in their place. The Changed entry on withdrawing alone gives the rules for the swap.
    - The new event [`MemberWithdrewEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MemberWithdrewEvent] reports each swap, with the code `battle.member.withdrew` for a fighting withdrawal and `battle.member.retreated` for a retreat. Its `character_id` is the member who stepped back and its `replacement_id` is the member who stepped up, so a battle screen can redraw the ranks from it.
    - The field defaults to `None`, so a saved command without it still parses and [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4.
- Each attack routine has a new field, [`AttackRoutine.use`][osrlib.core.monsters.AttackRoutine.use], that labels when the monster uses the routine. If you define monster templates of your own, label every routine after the first. An unlabelled routine is `PRIMARY` by default. The default policy, [`ScriptedPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.ScriptedPolicy], picks with equal chance among a monster's `PRIMARY` and `ALTERNATIVE` routines, and never picks a `SITUATIONAL` or `SPECIAL` one. So it picks an unlabelled routine as often as the first, even when it's a breath, a gaze, or a spell. A policy of your own can name any routine. A policy is an [`ActionPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.ActionPolicy], the object that chooses what a monster group does each round. The session uses the default policy for any group that has no policy of yours in [`GameSession.action_policies`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.action_policies]. Every routine in the built-in monster and spell catalogs has its label. The value is a [`RoutineUse`][osrlib.core.monsters.RoutineUse]:
    - `PRIMARY` for the first routine.
    - `ALTERNATIVE` when the OSE SRD lets the monster use the routine in place of the first in any round, like a thoul's weapon.
    - `SITUATIONAL` when the SRD ties the routine to a situation osrlib doesn't model, like an elephant's trample or a giant's thrown boulder.
    - `SPECIAL` for a breath, a gaze, or a spell.
- A policy of your own can choose which attack routine a monster uses, with the new field [`MonsterAction.routine`][osrlib.crawl.battle.MonsterAction.routine]. The field is an index into the template's `attacks`, and `None` uses the first routine. For example, your policy can have an elephant trample when your game decides it charges. Battle rolls the routine's attacks as melee attacks. A `SPECIAL` routine does nothing that turn, because battle rolls nothing for it. If the template has no routine at that index, the session skips the action without an error or an event, so the monster doesn't attack that turn.
- [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration] has a new field [`gaze_defense`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.gaze_defense], so a member can guard against a basilisk's or a medusa's petrifying gaze. Set it to `"avert_eyes"` or `"mirror"`, alongside whatever action the member declares. The defense lasts only the round it's declared in, and it protects the member whether the monsters or the party act first. The penalties come from the basilisk's and the medusa's entries in the OSE SRD.
    - A member who averts their eyes doesn't meet the gaze. Every attack roll they make that round takes a −4 penalty, and a basilisk or a medusa gains +2 to attack them. The member has the `averted_eyes` condition until the round ends.
    - A member who fights by a mirror doesn't meet the gaze either. Every attack roll they make that round takes a −1 penalty, and no monster gains a bonus against them. The member has to carry a `mirror`, or the session refuses the whole `ResolveBattleRound` command with the new code `battle.declaration.no_mirror`.
    - The defense makes no draw and emits no event.
    - On the monster side, the basilisk's and the medusa's `averting_eyes` abilities, which give those penalties, are no longer `manual`. A [`MonsterAbility`][osrlib.core.monsters.MonsterAbility] marked `manual` is text for a referee to act on, and osrlib doesn't apply it.
    - The field defaults to `None`, so a saved command without it still parses and [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4.
- The new event [`DeclarationFizzledEvent`][osrlib.core.events.DeclarationFizzledEvent] reports a device use or a turning that fizzled in a battle round (#145). A device is a wand, staff, or rod, and a turning is a cleric's `turn_undead`. Tell the player the action failed. Its code is `magic.device.fizzled` for a device and `magic.turning.fizzled` for a turning. `actor_id` is the member who declared it, `item_id` is the device's instance id (`None` for a turning), and `reason` is the rejection code of the check that failed. A device that fizzles doesn't use up a charge.

### Changed

- [`SpawnMonsters`][osrlib.crawl.commands.SpawnMonsters] now works during an encounter or a battle, so a game can add the allies a monster summons mid-fight, like a lycanthrope's animals or a nixie's fish (#170). Before, the session refused it with `session.command.encounter_in_progress`. To show the new group, handle the new event [`EncounterGroupJoinedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.EncounterGroupJoinedEvent] (`encounter.group.joined`), which gives the group's id, label, size, and distance. Also handle the new refusal code `encounter.spawn.mid_pursuit`, described below.
    - The command adds one new group to the open encounter at `distance_feet`. The group rolls no surprise or reaction, and it shares the encounter's stance, the monsters' attitude toward the party that the reaction roll set. It fights on the monster side from the next round.
    - The spawn takes no time and runs no round.
    - A monster that regenerates, like a troll, starts to regenerate as it joins.
    - During a pursuit, the session refuses the spawn with `encounter.spawn.mid_pursuit`, as it refuses a parley or a turning, because a chase runs at the pace of its slowest group.
    - The session logs the spawn like any other command, so loading a save or replaying the log reproduces it.
    - When a trigger's `SpawnMonsters` consequence fires during an encounter, its monsters now join that encounter. Before, the interpreter dropped the consequence with a note.
    - In exploration, the command opens an encounter as before. The session still refuses `SpawnNpcParty` and `PlaceParty` during an encounter.
- Turning the undead now ends a cleric's concentration (#167). A concentration spell is one whose effect lasts only while the caster concentrates on it. A cleric who declares `turn_undead` in [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] stops maintaining a concentration spell, as with any action other than `cast` or `hold`. Before, a turning that confusion stopped left the spell running.
- A caster now maintains one concentration spell at a time (#167). Casting a concentration spell in battle ends the one the caster already maintains. Before, both kept running. A spell that needs no concentration still leaves the maintained one alone.
- *Cure light wounds* cast to cure paralysis now removes the `paralysed` condition however the target got it (#166). Before, it released only effects attached through the [`EffectsLedger`][osrlib.core.effects.EffectsLedger]. So a target that a game paralysed with [`grant_condition`][osrlib.core.effects.grant_condition] stayed paralysed, and the cast reported no effect. The spell still removes no other condition.
- The temple now refuses a healing service that would do nothing, and takes no payment for it (#167). The session rejects [`PurchaseHealing`][osrlib.crawl.commands.PurchaseHealing] with the new code `town.healing.no_effect` for `raise_dead` on a living member, `cure_disease` on a member who isn't diseased, or `neutralize_poison` on a member who isn't poisoned. Before, the party paid 1,500 gp to raise a living member and the spell did nothing. A party that can't pay the fee still gets `items.purchase.insufficient_funds` first.
- A chute trap now drops the party only when at least one member fails the save (#167). A chute is a trap whose [`TrapEffect`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.TrapEffect] has a `transition`, which slides the party to another cell (one square of a level's grid) or to another level. When every save passes, the party stays on the trapped cell. Before, a chute moved the party whatever the saves rolled. A chute with no save still always drops the party, and the session rolls the saves as before.
- A melee attack on a petrified creature now hits automatically, as on a paralysed or sleeping one (#166). [`attack_roll`][osrlib.core.combat.attack_roll] reports `combat.attack.auto_hit` and draws no d20, so only the damage is rolled. A missile attack on a petrified creature is still rolled.
- [`UseItem`][osrlib.crawl.commands.UseItem] now refuses a potion whose `target_id` names someone other than the member using it, with `items.use.target_not_allowed` (#174). Before, the session accepted the command and the member in `character_id` drank the potion, so a front end that expected the target to drink got no warning. A `target_id` of `None`, or the id of the member using the potion, drinks the potion as before. A healing device still takes another member as its target.
- A plain [`Rest`][osrlib.crawl.commands.Rest] in a dungeon is now riskier: it checks for wandering monsters at the level's full chance. If your game wants the lower chance that every rest had in 2.0.1, set `hidden=True` on the `Rest` command, and pass `hidden=True` to `advance_turns` if you call it yourself. Before, every rest lowered the chance by 1, so a level with the usual 1 in 6 chance never rolled during a rest, and resting was always safe.
    - The session still raises the chance by 1 for noise and by 1 for bright light.
    - A rest of a night or a day, `Rest(kind="night")` or `Rest(kind="day")`, still checks on the level's usual schedule, every two turns unless the level sets another interval, and stops when monsters come.
    - [`GameSession.advance_turns`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.advance_turns] lowers the chance only when you pass `hidden=True`. `advance_turns(resting=True)` alone no longer lowers it, though it still counts the turns as rest.
    - The `resting` keyword of [`wandering_check`][osrlib.crawl.exploration.wandering_check] still lowers the chance by 1. `advance_turns` now sets it from `hidden`.
- A party member can now make a fighting withdrawal or a retreat alone, to pull a badly hurt character out of the front rank without the whole party leaving the fight (#171). The code `battle.declaration.formation_split` is gone. If your game handled it, handle the three new refusal codes instead: `battle.declaration.no_replacement`, `battle.declaration.replacement_unavailable`, and `battle.declaration.replacement_too_far`. The first item below says when the session uses each. Before, [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] used it to refuse a round in which only some of the members it expects to declare, the [`EncounterView.declarers`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.declarers], chose one of these moves.
    - The session refuses a member's move alone with one of these codes:
        - `battle.declaration.not_in_front_rank` when the member stands behind the front rank.
        - `battle.declaration.no_replacement` when the declaration names no `replacement_id`.
        - `battle.declaration.replacement_unavailable` when the named member can't step up.
        - `battle.declaration.replacement_too_far` when either member can't cover the distance between their ranks.
    - When every declarer makes the same fighting withdrawal or retreat, the whole party makes it, as before. Any other fighting withdrawal or retreat is that member's alone.
    - The member has to stand in the front rank. They step back and trade places in the marching order with the member named in [`BattleDeclaration.replacement_id`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.replacement_id]. The party doesn't move and the battle goes on.
    - Each rank stands 5 feet behind the one in front of it, and both members have to cover that distance. The withdrawing member covers it at up to half their encounter movement rate for a fighting withdrawal, or their full rate for a retreat. The replacement covers it at up to their full rate. *Haste* doubles either limit for the member who has it.
    - The swap happens in the party's movement step, so monsters that win initiative strike at the ranks as they were declared. From the swap on, melee no longer reaches the member who stepped back.
    - The replacement may declare a melee attack in the round they step up, and it resolves only if the swap happens.
    - A member who retreats alone is easier to hit for the whole round: attackers get +2 against them and ignore the retreating member's shield.
    - The new order stays after the battle, until a [`ReorderParty`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ReorderParty] sets it again. The session still refuses `ReorderParty` during a battle.
    - A refused round costs no draw and no time, and a swap makes no draw.
- An `attack` in a [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration] with no `weapon_id` now uses the wielded weapon that suits the attack (#162). Before, the member struck unarmed, even with a sword in hand. A declaration that names a weapon works as before. No declaration asks for an unarmed strike, so a member who wields a suitable weapon can't strike unarmed. [`BattleDeclaration.weapon_id`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.weapon_id] describes the choice.
    - The distance to the target group when the session accepts the round decides the attack: melee at 5 feet or less, missile beyond.
    - The session takes the first wielded weapon that can make that attack, in the order the member equipped them. For a missile attack, it prefers the first whose long range reaches the group.
    - A real weapon comes before an improvised one, like a torch, so a member who lit a torch and then drew a sword swings the sword. The torch still counts when the member wields nothing else that suits the attack.
    - The session passes over a splash weapon, like a flask of oil, and an item with no weapon stats, like a wand.
    - A member with no suitable weapon still strikes unarmed.
    - The round plays out as if you had named the chosen weapon, and the attack events name the weapon the member used. The command log records the declaration as you sent it.
- A device use or a turning declared in [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] is now checked again in the magic phase, just before it resolves, as a spell cast and a scroll read are (#145). The magic phase is the step of a battle round in which spells, devices, and turnings resolve. A declaration that fails the second check fizzles, and the session reports it with a [`DeclarationFizzledEvent`][osrlib.core.events.DeclarationFizzledEvent], so handle that event. Before, these declarations resolved even when the round had changed something they depend on. For example, a staff of striking struck a group that had already run out of melee reach.
    - A device that fizzles was never used. It doesn't use up a charge, the session doesn't identify it, and the member who declared it stays invisible if they were.
    - A turning that fizzles rolls nothing.
    - A device use or a turning that passes the second check resolves with the same rolls as before.
- A party turned wholly to stone now ends the game, as a party that has died does. Handle it as you handle a dead party: the session moves to `game_over` and reports a [`GameOverEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.GameOverEvent]. Before, a statue counted as alive, so a party that a basilisk's gaze turned to stone stayed in the encounter or in a battle with nobody able to act.
    - A member stands while they are neither dead nor petrified. When a command kills or petrifies a member and no member is left standing, the session ends the game, and the `GameOverEvent` comes last. The ending makes no draws.
    - In a battle, `battle.ended.defeat` comes just before the ending.
    - When a basilisk's surprise gaze turns the whole party to stone as an encounter opens, the monsters get no round against the statues.
    - A statue still counts among [`Party.living_members`][osrlib.crawl.party.Party.living_members] and keeps its rank, because a member turned to stone isn't dead. Only the check that ends the game treats a statue as fallen.
    - Your game can stop at the ending, or offer a way back. The temple can't cure stone, so the way back is a magic-user who casts *stone to flesh* in town. [`PlaceParty`][osrlib.crawl.commands.PlaceParty] is legal in `game_over`, so first place the party in town with it, which doesn't end the game again. Then add the magic-user with [`AddMember`][osrlib.crawl.commands.AddMember] and cast the spell with [`CastSpell`][osrlib.crawl.commands.CastSpell].
- Some command logs recorded under 2.0.1 replay differently under this release. To continue a 2.0.1 game, load its save with [`load_game`][osrlib.persistence.load_game]. A load restores the saved state and runs no commands, so this change doesn't affect it. The loaded game then plays on under this release's rules. To reproduce a 2.0.1 game exactly from its log, install `osrlib==2.0.1` in a separate environment and replay the log there. To catch a log from another version before it replays, pass the save's `engine_version` to [`replay_game`][osrlib.persistence.replay_game] as `recorded_engine_version`. The call then raises [`ReplayVersionError`][osrlib.errors.ReplayVersionError] when the versions differ. Replaying a 2.0.1 log under this release can differ in two ways. Each case below matches an entry in the Changed or Fixed section of these notes.
    - The replay raises [`ContentValidationError`][osrlib.errors.ContentValidationError] when the log contains a command this release refuses. These commands that 2.0.1 accepted are refused now:
        - A potion `UseItem` aimed at another member.
        - A temple service that would do nothing.
        - A battle round in which a member behind the front rank strikes with a staff of striking.
        - A battle round that declares a `close` move (an order to move toward a group) or an area spell at a group that has fled, that is, run out of the battle.
        - A command after the whole party was turned to stone, unless `game_over` mode allows it. The session now ends the game when the last member turns to stone, so it refuses most later commands.
    - The replay runs without an error but plays out differently when the log contains any of these:
        - A lair the party left and came back to.
        - A rest in a dungeon, or a rest that a wandering monster interrupted.
        - A trap save with a `modifier`, or a chute trap where every member passed the save.
        - A drained monster met by *sleep*, a *confusion* re-save, a protection scroll, or the 5 Hit Dice rule.
        - A *death spell* cast at a group.
        - A monster that regenerates (a troll, a thoul, or a vampire).
        - A monster whose attack has an effect, like a ghoul's paralysing claw or a wight's level drain.
        - A basilisk's or a medusa's gaze, including a basilisk that surprises the party, whether or not a battle follows.
        - A melee attack on a petrified creature.
        - A spitting cobra's spit that hits.
        - A ghoul or a thoul that attacks while one member in its reach is paralysed and another isn't.
        - A monster or an NPC adventurer that attacks while one member in its reach is turned to stone and another isn't.
        - A monster that can choose between attack routines: a spitting cobra, a thoul, a mule, a minotaur, a wererat, or a monster of your own with more than one unlabelled routine.
        - An attack declared with no `weapon_id` by a member who wields a weapon.
        - A wand, staff, rod, or turning that now fizzles.
        - A round in which the whole party retreats and a monster attacks a member before the party moves off, or while the monster is confused.
        - A turning after a monster in the encounter died or its group fled the battle.
        - A staff of striking used by a member who was to step up into the front rank but whose swap didn't happen.
        - A level drain on a character, whose drain event now reports different `hp_lost` and `spawn_consequence` values. The drain itself takes the same levels and hit points.

### Fixed

- When the whole party retreats, every attack the monster side makes on the party that round now gets +2 and ignores the shield, whichever side acts first. That's what the OSE SRD's retreat says: "This round: ... the opponent gains a +2 bonus to all attacks against the retreating combatant and ignores any AC bonus due to the combatant's shield". Before, the penalty started only when the party moved off, so monsters that won initiative and attacked first got no bonus and counted the shield. A confused monster's attack never got it.
- A ring of regeneration no longer heals fire or acid damage, as the ring's entry in the OSE SRD says. Before, the ring healed every kind of damage at 1 hit point a round.
    - [`deal_damage`][osrlib.core.combat.deal_damage] records fire and acid damage to the wearer in the new field [`Character.nonregen_damage`][osrlib.core.character.Character.nonregen_damage]. The ring heals the wearer only up to their maximum hit points less that damage. The ring still heals the rest, and a potion or a cure spell still heals all of it.
    - [`DamageDealtEvent`][osrlib.core.events.DamageDealtEvent]`.non_regenerable` is now True when fire or acid hits a wearer.
    - The new field defaults to 0, so a save from before this change still loads and [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4. Saves and party documents now include the field.
- *Sleep* now judges a drained monster by the Hit Dice it has left, because energy drain takes Hit Dice away. Before, both of the spell's modes read the monster's template. Take an ogre drained from 4+1 to 3+1 Hit Dice as the example:
    - The single-creature mode takes only a creature of exactly 4+1 Hit Dice: 4 Hit Dice with a bonus, no more and no fewer. The drained ogre is no longer a 4+1 creature, so this mode now has no effect on it. Before, the mode put that ogre to sleep.
    - The 2d8 Hit Dice mode affects as many creatures as a 2d8 roll of Hit Dice, its budget, pays for. It takes creatures of 4 Hit Dice or fewer and leaves out 4+1 creatures. It now takes the drained ogre for 3 Hit Dice of its budget. Before, it left the ogre out as a 4+1 creature.
    - This fix covers the 4+1 test in both modes. The [`effective_hd`][osrlib.core.combat.effective_hd] entry, later in this section, covers the rest of the 2d8 mode: what each creature costs from the budget, and whether it's under the 4 Hit Dice limit. With that fix, a 5 Hit Dice grizzly bear drained to 4 is eligible for the 2d8 mode too.
- Three more rules now judge a drained monster by the Hit Dice it has left, because energy drain takes Hit Dice away. Before, each used the monster's template. The count these rules use is [`MonsterInstance.hit_dice_count`][osrlib.core.monsters.MonsterInstance.hit_dice_count], the same count that sets the monster's THAC0 and saves. The turning table and a monster's experience value still use the template, because the OSE SRD ties them to the kind of monster. The three rules:
    - *Confusion*'s re-save each round, which a monster gets when it has 3 Hit Dice or more, or 2 Hit Dice with a bonus, like 2+1. A 3 Hit Dice boar drained to 2 Hit Dice is a subject of 2 Hit Dice and no longer gets the re-save.
    - The Hit Dice bands of the protection scrolls. A 4 Hit Dice wraith drained to 3 Hit Dice counts in the 1 to 3 Hit Dice band of a scroll of protection from undead.
    - The optional rule that lets a monster of 5 Hit Dice or more harm a creature that only magic or silver can hurt. A grizzly drained from 5 to 4 Hit Dice no longer qualifies.
- *Death spell* cast at a group with `target_group_id` now takes only the creatures its 60' cube covers. Before, the cast took every living member of the group. The cast now uses the same [area footprint](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/adaptations/#the-area-footprint) as any other area spell in [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration]: the group's living members in the order the session spawned them, up to the number of creatures the area can cover. The party's front rank comes next when the `aoe_friendly_fire` option of the [`Ruleset`][osrlib.core.ruleset.Ruleset] is on, the group is within 5 feet of the party, and the cube has room left. The option is on by default. With the option off, the cube never takes party members. A cast with named `targets` still uses those targets. *Sleep* has no area in the OSE SRD, so it still takes every living member of the group.
- When a wandering monster interrupts a rest, the full turns the party rested before the monsters came now count (#169). Before, a 48-turn night broken after four full turns cleared neither fatigue nor exhaustion. Now one full turn of rest clears fatigue. Each full turn also counts toward the three that clear exhaustion from a chase, and the next rest adds to that count. A rest interrupted during its first turn still clears nothing. An interrupted night still isn't sleep for preparing spells, and an interrupted day still heals nobody. The fix makes no new draws.
- Trolls, thouls, and vampires now regenerate in play (#167). Until this release a spawned one never healed, though its template had the ability. A monster whose template has the `regeneration` ability now starts to regenerate when its encounter opens, whether it came from a [`SpawnMonsters`][osrlib.crawl.commands.SpawnMonsters] command, a keyed area, or a wandering-monster check. The command that opened the encounter reports an [`EffectAttachedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.EffectAttachedEvent] for each such monster. A game recorded under 2.0.1 that met one of these monsters replays differently under this release, as the replay entry under Changed describes.
    - A troll's regeneration attaches when the encounter opens, but a hurt troll heals only from 3 rounds after it last took damage. Then it gets back 3 hit points a round. It never gets back damage from fire or acid.
    - A troll slain while its encounter is still running gets back up 2d6 rounds later and fights again. It stays dead if fire and acid alone did as much damage as its maximum hit points. The session rolls the revival delay on the effects stream ([`EFFECTS_STREAM`][osrlib.core.effects.EFFECTS_STREAM]).
    - A hurt thoul gets back 1 hit point, and a hurt vampire 3 hit points, at the start of each round while it lives.
    - The session releases the effect when the encounter ends, so a troll that is still dead then stays dead.
- Undead that a cleric turns now flee and attack no one, even when the cleric turns only some of a group (#168). Before, the turned members of a partly turned group kept closing and attacking, the cleric who turned them included. Now they break away as a new group, with a new group id, which runs from the battle the way a wholly turned group does. The new group needs no policy from your game. Each round, the session moves it away at its full movement rate and reports the move in a [`GroupMovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.GroupMovedEvent]. Once it's more than [`FLEE_EXIT_FEET`][osrlib.crawl.battle.FLEE_EXIT_FEET] (120 feet) away, it has fled the battle. The session reports the split in the middle of the battle with a [`MonsterFledEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MonsterFledEvent] whose `group_id` is the new group's id, so add that group to your battle screen when you see the event.
    - The members who weren't turned stay in the original group and fight on. A policy you registered for that group still chooses their actions.
    - The group's shared treasure stays with the members who fight on. Each turned member takes only the treasure it carries.
    - A turned monster that can't move, like one caught in a *web*, can't run away. It stays where it is and does nothing.
    - The session skips any action a policy returns for a turned monster.
    - A wholly turned group behaves as before.
- When a monster's attack hits in battle, the session now applies the effects the attack lists (#167). In a battle against one of these monsters, the session makes more draws from the combat, effects, and advancement streams ([`COMBAT_STREAM`][osrlib.core.combat.COMBAT_STREAM], `EFFECTS_STREAM`, and [`ADVANCEMENT_STREAM`][osrlib.core.character.ADVANCEMENT_STREAM]). So the same seed and commands give a different battle than they gave under 2.0.1. The [adaptations register](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/adaptations/), the docs page that records where osrlib interprets or adapts the rules, lists the attack effects that the session leaves to the referee.
    - A ghoul's or a thoul's claw paralyses for 2d4 turns unless the target saves versus paralysis. An elf isn't affected.
    - A carcass crawler's tentacle and a gelatinous cube's touch paralyse the same way, and they affect elves too.
    - A poisonous bite or sting kills unless the target saves versus death. The target dies at once, or when the poison's onset runs out if the monster's poison has one. The session records the death as a poison death, so *neutralize poison* can revive the character.
    - A mummy's touch gives mummy rot, with no save.
    - A wight's, a wraith's, a spectre's, or a vampire's touch drains levels, also with no save.
    - A basilisk's bite and a cockatrice's beak turn the target to stone unless it saves versus petrification.
    - The basilisk's and the medusa's petrifying gaze reaches each character in the party's front rank once a round while the gazer is in melee. The gaze takes no attack roll, and the session no longer rolls the basilisk's gaze as an attack.
    - A character with the `averted_eyes` condition doesn't meet the gaze, and neither does a member who sets the new [`BattleDeclaration.gaze_defense`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.gaze_defense] field. A blind character can't meet a gaze, so [`resolve_gaze`][osrlib.core.combat.resolve_gaze] spares one too.
- When a basilisk surprises the party, every member now meets its gaze as the encounter opens, as the OSE SRD's basilisk says: "Characters surprised by a basilisk meet its gaze." Each living member, back rank included, makes one saving throw versus paralysis, which in B/X also covers petrification, or turns to stone. Before, a basilisk that surprised the party gazed only once it was in melee, and then only at the front rank.
    - A blind member can't meet the gaze and makes no save.
    - The gaze comes at whatever distance the encounter opened and whatever the monsters' reaction, so it comes in an encounter that never becomes a battle. It comes even when the basilisk is also surprised, because a gaze takes no action.
    - The saving throws come among the opening events of [`start_encounter`][osrlib.crawl.encounter.start_encounter], so the command that opened the encounter reports them. That's true whether the encounter came from a [`SpawnMonsters`][osrlib.crawl.commands.SpawnMonsters] command, a keyed area, or a wandering-monster check.
    - A battle that opens from the encounter doesn't repeat the gaze. A side that surprises the other gets a free round to act before the other side can. When the basilisk attacks at once, it doesn't gaze again in its free round, so a member in melee meets its gaze once.
    - A medusa doesn't do this, because her entry has no such rule.
    - In the monster catalog, the basilisk's `surprise` ability is no longer `manual`, and its `params`, the values osrlib reads from the ability, include `meets_gaze: true`. You don't need to set either.
- Battle now uses a monster's other attack routines, not only the first. Before, it used only the first routine in the monster's stat block. So a thoul never used a weapon and a mule never bit, an effect in a later routine never applied, and not even a policy of your own could make an elephant trample. Now the default policy, [`ScriptedPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.ScriptedPolicy], picks a routine for each melee attack, with equal chance among the first routine and the alternatives the OSE SRD offers.
    - Each round, the spitting cobra either spits or bites, and its bite poisons. The thoul either claws or swings a weapon, the mule kicks or bites, the minotaur gores and bites or swings a weapon, and the wererat bites or uses a weapon.
    - The default policy never picks a situational routine, like an elephant's trample. A policy of your own can name one, and battle then rolls it as a melee attack.
    - Battle never rolls a breath, a gaze, or a spell as an attack, even when a policy names one.
    - The policy draws the pick from the monster-action stream ([`MONSTER_ACTION_STREAM`][osrlib.crawl.session.MONSTER_ACTION_STREAM]), and only for a monster that has a choice. Every other monster draws as before.
- A spitting cobra's spit ability has a new tag, `blindness`, where it had `blinding_spit`, and a blind member can't attack. If your game looks the ability up with [`MonsterTemplate.ability`][osrlib.core.monsters.MonsterTemplate.ability], pass `"blindness"` now. [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] refuses an attack from a blind member with `combat.attack.attacker_blind`, so don't offer the attack. The spit now blinds, as the OSE SRD's snake entry says: "A hit causes permanent blindness (save vs poison)." Before, a spit that hit did nothing.
    - The target makes a saving throw versus death, which in B/X also covers poison, or is blind for good. The save draws from the combat stream, so a battle in which a spit hits plays out differently than before.
    - The refusal follows the SRD's combat rules, which say "A blind character is unable to attack".
    - The blindness is permanent, and *dispel magic* doesn't end it. No spell osrlib runs cures it, so a cure is the referee's call.
    - In the monster catalog, the spit ability is no longer `manual`.
- A ghoul or a thoul now turns from a member it has paralysed and attacks someone else, as the OSE SRD's ghoul entry says: "After paralysing a target, ghouls will attack others." The thoul's entry says the same of thouls. To give a monster of your own this behavior, set the new param `attacks_others: true` in its `paralysis` ability. Before, the default policy, [`ScriptedPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.ScriptedPolicy], picked a paralysed member as often as any other.
    - The policy passes over a paralysed member while someone who is neither paralysed nor turned to stone is in reach. It attacks a paralysed member only when no one else is in reach, and it attacks a paralysed member before a statue.
    - The policy picks a target once a round, so the rest of a ghoul's attacks that round stay on a member its claw has just paralysed. The ghoul turns to someone else from the next round.
    - The policy reads `attacks_others` in the monster's `paralysis` ability, not the monster's id. In the monster catalog, the ghoul's and the thoul's `paralysis` abilities have `attacks_others: true`.
    - A carcass crawler and a gelatinous cube still attack the members they paralyse, because their entries don't say otherwise.
    - When a ghoul or a thoul passes over a paralysed member, its pick from the monster-action stream changes, so that fight plays out differently than before.
- A monster or an NPC adventurer no longer attacks a member who was turned to stone while someone else is in reach. Before, the default policies, [`ScriptedPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.ScriptedPolicy] for monsters and [`NpcPartyPolicy`][osrlib.crawl.battle.NpcPartyPolicy] for NPC adventurers, picked a statue as often as any other member of the front rank, so a basilisk kept biting the members it had already turned to stone.
    - A statue is out of the fight, so both policies now pass over one while a member who isn't stone is in reach. A monster attacks a paralysed member before a statue. When every member in reach is stone, the attacker picks a statue, as before.
    - The SRD doesn't say this, so the adaptations register records it as a judgement.
    - The draws from the monster-action stream are the same as before for a monster with no paralysed or petrified member in reach, for an NPC adventurer with no statue in reach, and for either one with only statues in reach.
- A trap's saving throw now adds the `modifier` of the trap's [`SaveSpec`][osrlib.core.spells.SaveSpec], as a spell's save does (#167). Before, the session left the modifier out, so a trap you wrote with a bonus or a penalty on its save played as if it had none. The modifier changes whether the save passes. The dice the session rolls are the same as before.
- Lighting the last oil flask in a pile on the floor with [`LightSource`][osrlib.crawl.commands.LightSource] no longer deletes the gems, jewellery, or magic items still in the pile (#167). A pile, a [`DropPile`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DropPile], is the loose goods lying on one cell's floor. Before, the session removed the pile once it had no ordinary items and no coins. Now it leaves the pile while gems, jewellery, or magic items are still in it.
- When a breath weapon or *disintegrate* kills a party member, the magic items that survive now go to a drop pile on the party's cell in the same command (#167). To recover them, send [`TakeTreasure`][osrlib.crawl.commands.TakeTreasure] with the feature id `"pile"`. If a pile is already on the cell, the items join it. Before, the items stayed in the dead member's inventory. The commands that hand over or drop an item refuse a dead member, so the party couldn't recover them. [`EquipmentDestroyedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.EquipmentDestroyedEvent]`.saved_items` still lists them, and the draws are unchanged.
- The lair hoard of a [`KeyedEncounter`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.KeyedEncounter], the monsters an adventure places in a keyed area, is now rolled once (#167). A lair hoard is the treasure in the monsters' lair. The session rolls it the first time the party walks in, and puts it down as a cache, treasure on a cell that the party takes with [`TakeTreasure`][osrlib.crawl.commands.TakeTreasure]. Before, a party that evaded the monsters, left, and came back made the session roll the hoard again and put down a second cache, so walking in and out of a lair produced more treasure each time. Now the monsters spawn again, but the party finds the cache from the first visit, or nothing if it took that cache.
    - The new [`DungeonState.generated_lair_hoards`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DungeonState.generated_lair_hoards] list records the lairs whose hoard was rolled. It defaults to empty, so a save from before this change still loads and [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4.
    - A save from before this change has no record of the hoards already rolled. After you load it, the session rolls the hoard once more for a lair that the party visited before the save and whose monsters it didn't clear. That happens the next time the party walks in, even if the party took the first cache. The session records that roll, so it doesn't roll the hoard again.
- The players now see a gate's `success` text when the gate is on a passage or stairs that lead to another cell of the same level (#167). A [gate](https://mmacy.github.io/osrlib-python/guides/gates-triggers-quests/#gating-a-door-or-a-stair), a [`GateSpec`][osrlib.crawl.gates.GateSpec], is a condition the party must meet to get through a door, a stair, or a passage. If your front end builds the line for a [`PartyMovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.PartyMovedEvent] instead of calling [`format_message`][osrlib.messages.format_message], show the event's `narrative`. Before, the session put the text only on a level or dungeon entry, which it doesn't report for a crossing within one level, so the text never appeared.
    - The text is in the `narrative` of the `PartyMovedEvent` for the landing cell, the cell where the party arrives, and `format_message` adds it to that event's line.
    - For stairs within one level, the session emits that event only when the gate has success text.
    - `PartyMovedEvent.narrative` is a new field. It's `None` when the gate has no success text and when no gate was crossed. It defaults to `None`, so [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4.
- Reading a door's state with [`DungeonState.door`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DungeonState.door] no longer changes the door (#159). Before, the first call for a door stored an all-`False` entry. So a front end that read an untouched door whose [`DoorSpec`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DoorSpec] sets `starts_open` left it shut for the rest of play, and a save recorded it as shut. Now the method stores nothing for a read. For an untouched door, it returns a new state that starts from the door's `DoorSpec`, so `open` reads `True` when `starts_open` is set and every other field reads `False`. Setting a field on that state stores the entry and changes only that field, so `door(ref).discovered = True` on a door that starts open stores a door that is open and discovered, and the party can still walk through. A `DungeonState` that no session holds has no adventure to read, so for it a new state still reads `False` in every field. To show doors as the party sees them, read the player view from [`GameSession.view`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.view].
- [`end_encounter`][osrlib.crawl.encounter.end_encounter] now raises `ValueError` when no encounter is open. Before, it raised `AttributeError`. It raises before it changes the session or draws from a random number stream, so a refused call leaves the game as it was.
- Worn gauntlets of ogre power now raise the wearer's carrying capacity under detailed encumbrance, as their entry in the OSE SRD says (#166). A game uses detailed encumbrance when its [`Ruleset`][osrlib.core.ruleset.Ruleset] sets `encumbrance` to `EncumbranceMode.DETAILED`. That mode sets each character's movement rate from bands of carried weight. Each movement band and the maximum load rise by the template's `max_load_bonus_coins`, 1,000 coins. So the wearer moves at 120' up to 1,400 coins, not 400, and can still move at 2,600 coins, the raised maximum. When the party takes treasure with [`TakeTreasure`][osrlib.crawl.commands.TakeTreasure], the engine loads the wearer up to that raised maximum. Basic encumbrance is unchanged, because the entry gives the extra weight under detailed encumbrance only.
- [`damage_roll`][osrlib.core.combat.damage_roll] now adds an enchanted weapon's `damage_bonus` (#166). Before, it added the `attack_bonus`. Every built-in weapon has equal bonuses and deals the same damage as before. A weapon made from a custom template whose two bonuses differ now adds its damage bonus. A matching versus clause's `bonus` still replaces the damage bonus.
- [`effective_hd`][osrlib.core.combat.effective_hd] now counts the Hit Dice a drained monster has left (#166). Before, it counted the template's, so a drained ogre still counted as 4 Hit Dice. *Sleep*'s 2d8 mode uses this count for what each creature costs from its budget and for its 4 Hit Dice limit. The entry "*Sleep* now judges a drained monster by the Hit Dice it has left", earlier in this section, covers the spell's test for a 4+1 creature.
- A spent stack, a magic item at quantity 0 like an emptied staff, now weighs nothing (#166). Before, [`treasure_weight_coins`][osrlib.core.items.treasure_weight_coins] counted it as one item, though [`Inventory.carried_item`][osrlib.core.items.Inventory.carried_item] reported none carried, so it could slow the member who had it.
- When an enchanted item's instance has no `base_item_id`, every function now reads the template's base (#166). Before, [`equipment_weight_coins`][osrlib.core.items.equipment_weight_coins] and the movement rate of worn armour read only the instance's field. So a shield +1 built without it weighed nothing, and enchanted armour whose template sets its base moved as unarmoured. Items made by [`instantiate_magic_item`][osrlib.core.treasure.instantiate_magic_item] have their base on the instance and aren't affected.
- The [`RollResult`][osrlib.core.dice.RollResult] that [`damage_roll`][osrlib.core.combat.damage_roll] returns now obeys its documented rule, `total == (sum(rolls) + modifier) * multiplier`, before the total is raised to 1 (#166). Before, `modifier` was always 0 and `multiplier` was always 1, so a front end that showed the breakdown showed the wrong numbers. Totals and draw sequences are unchanged.
    - `modifier` includes the Strength modifier, the magic weapon's bonus, spell bonuses, and a fixed amount.
    - `multiplier` includes the doublings, like a back-stab or a braced spear.
- A front end built on the player view can now cast *sleep* at a group in battle (#162). Set `target_group_id` and leave `targets` empty. Before, the session refused that with `magic.cast.target_count`, and the player view shows no monster ids to name instead.
    - This works for any `hd_budget` mode, a mode that affects as many creatures as a rolled pool of Hit Dice pays for. When a [`BattleDeclaration`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration] for such a mode has a `target_group_id` and no `targets`, the session collects that group's living members, in the order it spawned them, as the candidates. The spell then spends its budget on the candidates with the fewest Hit Dice first, until the budget can't pay for the next one.
    - For *sleep*, which has no area, the candidates are every living member of the group, so it's the same cast as one that lists them all in `targets`. A mode with an area, like *death spell*'s 60' cube, takes only the creatures the area covers.
    - When both are set, the session still uses the named `targets`, so a saved command log replays as it did.
- A front end can now tell that a monster group has fled, and the session refuses every declaration aimed at a fled group (#162). [`EncounterGroupView`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterGroupView] has a new `fled` field, which is true once the group has left the battle. If your front end lists groups as targets, leave out the groups whose `fled` is true. A fled group stays in the player view's `groups`, in order.
    - Before, the session refused an attack or an item aimed at a fled group but accepted a `close` move or an area spell. Now the session refuses each of these with `battle.declaration.unknown_group`, with the declaring member's id as the `character` param.
    - You can still declare against a group that is running away but hasn't left the battle.
    - A spell aimed at a group that flees earlier in the same round fizzles in the magic phase, as a spell does when any of its checks fails there.
    - The field defaults to `False`, so [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4.
- A turning now reaches only the monsters still in the fight: the living monsters of the groups that haven't fled. Before, a `turn_undead` in [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] reached every monster in the encounter, the dead and the fled included. A skeleton shot dead in the missile phase was still read on the turning table, so the turning could report `magic.turning.turned` with no monster affected. When the magic phase finds no such monster left, the turning fizzles with a [`DeclarationFizzledEvent`][osrlib.core.events.DeclarationFizzledEvent] at `magic.turning.fizzled`, whose `reason` is `battle.declaration.no_target`. A fizzled turning spends nothing and draws nothing, though it still ends the cleric's concentration. [`TurnUndead`][osrlib.crawl.commands.TurnUndead] before a battle skips a monster killed earlier in the encounter in the same way.
- Only a member in the front rank can now strike with a staff of striking, because the OSE SRD's entry says it's "Used as a magical melee weapon". If your front end offers the staff's use, offer it only to members in [`EncounterView.front_rank`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView.front_rank] and to a member who steps up this round. Before, a member in the back rank could use one in [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound], with a `BattleDeclaration` whose `action` is `"use_item"` and whose `item_id` is the staff's. Now the session refuses that use with `battle.declaration.not_in_front_rank`, as it refuses a melee attack.
    - A member who steps up this round, named in [`replacement_id`][osrlib.crawl.commands.BattleDeclaration.replacement_id], may strike. A side that acted first can stop the swap, for example by leaving either member unable to move, or the replacement asleep, paralysed, charmed, or confused. If the swap doesn't happen, the use fizzles in the magic phase at `magic.device.fizzled`, and the staff doesn't use up a charge.
    - With the ruleset's `formation_width_limit` off, every member stands in the front rank and may strike.
- The `hp_lost` of a [`LevelDrainedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.LevelDrainedEvent] is now how far the victim's maximum hit points fell. Before, it was the hit points rolled for the lost levels, which overstated the loss when the maximum stopped at 1. A level 2 dwarf with 3 maximum hit points who rolled a 7 for the lost level drops to 1 maximum hit point. The event reported a loss of 7, and now reports a loss of 2. The `hp_lost` of [`DrainResult`][osrlib.core.classes.DrainResult] is still the hit points rolled, so the two values can differ, and that's intended.
- The `spawn_consequence` of a [`LevelDrainedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.LevelDrainedEvent] now appears only on a `combat.drain.slain` event, because it gives what a character drained of all levels becomes. It's `None` on every other drain event. Before, a drain the character survived had it too, as if the character were about to rise as a wight.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-10-15

### Added

- [`osrlib.core.creature`][osrlib.core.creature] declares the three protocols a rules function reads its creature argument through: [`Creature`][osrlib.core.creature.Creature] (id, name, alignment, hit points, conditions, stat modifiers), [`Combatant`][osrlib.core.creature.Combatant] (the attack, initiative, and saving-throw numbers), and [`Caster`][osrlib.core.creature.Caster] (level, spell book, memorized spells) (#104). A [`Character`][osrlib.core.character.Character] satisfies all three and a [`MonsterInstance`][osrlib.core.monsters.MonsterInstance] the first two, structurally, so a caller passes either without a cast and annotates its own functions with the protocol that names what they read. Every public creature parameter in [`osrlib.core.combat`][osrlib.core.combat], [`osrlib.core.spells`][osrlib.core.spells], [`osrlib.core.effects`][osrlib.core.effects], and [`osrlib.core.items`][osrlib.core.items] now states the protocol the function reads through it, in place of `Any` and `object`: [`attack_roll`][osrlib.core.combat.attack_roll] takes a `Combatant` attacker and defender, [`has_condition`][osrlib.core.effects.has_condition] a `Creature`, [`cast_spell`][osrlib.core.spells.cast_spell] a `Caster` and a sequence of `Creature` values or the location strings a place-targeted spell takes, and a function that reads what one concrete type alone has takes that type, so [`resolve_breath`][osrlib.core.combat.resolve_breath] takes a `MonsterInstance` and [`sword_control_check`][osrlib.core.items.sword_control_check] a `Character`. The signature is what pyright checks a front end's call against and what the reference links. Runtime behaviour is unchanged, no rule, event, or draw sequence moved, and what changes is what a type checker accepts: a caller that passed an object without the protocol's attributes now fails its own pyright run. [`GameSession.combatant`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.combatant] returns `MonsterInstance | Character | None` rather than `object | None`, so a front end can hand its result straight to a rules function.
- [`StreamName`][osrlib.core.rng.StreamName] is the one home for every RNG stream key the library draws from, and each public `*_STREAM` constant takes its value from the matching member, so `COMBAT_STREAM` and `StreamName.COMBAT` are one object (#106). Name a stream through the enum rather than writing its key out: a misspelled key raises nothing, because it forks a stream of its own and draws plausible numbers from it, which is the one defect the determinism contract cannot catch. `StreamName` is a `StrEnum`, so a member is its own string and the values are unchanged: [`RngStreams`][osrlib.core.rng.RngStreams] keys by string, a save file records the same keys, and seed material and draw order are untouched.
- [`MoraleCheckedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.MoraleCheckedEvent]`.held` states whether the side keeps fighting, and [`check_morale`][osrlib.core.combat.check_morale] fills it on every code. `combat.morale.exempt` covers both exemptions, so a front end that wants to tell a side that never fights from one that never breaks reads `held` instead of pairing the code with the score. The default formatter's `combat.morale.exempt` line reads it, and falls back to the score for a log written before schema 4, where the field defaults `None`.
- The API reference has a front page for `osrlib.core` and for `osrlib.crawl`, at the top of each layer's section, rendering that package's docstring, so the crawl package's end-to-end program is on the published site (#101).
- Four event fields say where something came from, so a front end reads the fact off the event instead of rebuilding it from where the party is standing (#67, #68, #70, #71). [`LocationEnteredEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.LocationEnteredEvent]`.via` gives, on a level or dungeon entry, the transition's `kind` for [`UseStairs`][osrlib.crawl.commands.UseStairs], `"trap"` for a trap that dropped the party through the floor, `"entrance"` for [`EnterDungeon`][osrlib.crawl.commands.EnterDungeon], and `"placed"` for a referee's [`PlaceParty`][osrlib.crawl.commands.PlaceParty], and `.transition_ref` names the cell the transition stands on, as [`cell_ref`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.cell_ref] writes it, so a stairs crossing maps back to its [`TransitionSpec`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.TransitionSpec] and the gate on it, which is what a narrator needs to read the gate's `guidance` beside the success beat. [`ItemAcquiredEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.ItemAcquiredEvent]`.origin` says where goods came from, `"treasure"` for a share of a haul, `"purchase"` for gear bought in town, and `"grant"` for a referee's [`GrantItem`][osrlib.crawl.commands.GrantItem] or [`GrantCoins`][osrlib.crawl.commands.GrantCoins], so a reward counted out per member never reads as a pot split. A room trap found through a door names that door on both surfaces the find has: [`TrapEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.TrapEvent]`.direction` carries the bearing, and the search token takes a third segment, `room_trap:<area id>:<direction>`, while a trap found inside its own area keeps the bare token and no bearing. The engine always fills the four fields, and each defaults `None` so a log written before schema 4 still parses. The default formatter reads them, so a stairs crossing reads as the climb or the descent it was and a door-edge trap find names the door.
- An unidentified enchanted arm's public combat facts reach the player view, matching an identified one. [`MemberView`][osrlib.crawl.views.MemberView]'s masked inventory gives an unidentified magic item `qualities` and `missile_ranges` when its masked display string was built from that base weapon ("a dagger with a faint aura"). An item whose display comes from its category instead, such as a `staff_of_striking` reading "a staff", shows neither field, and neither does a shield or a ring. On the base-weapon path the two fields reveal nothing the display string does not already: they are rulebook facts about the mundane weapon underneath, not about the enchantment, and the bonus, any curse, the template id, and the true name all stay hidden. Without them a front end cannot tell a melee declaration from a missile one for an item it cannot yet name.
- A passage is a transition the party walks through instead of taking with [`UseStairs`][osrlib.crawl.commands.UseStairs]. Use one where a map shows two cells side by side that the grid can't put side by side, like the floors of two levels that meet, or two halves of one map that you lay out apart. A [`TransitionSpec`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.TransitionSpec] whose `kind` is `"passage"` names the `side` of its cell that it stands on, and that side is a wall in the level's edges. A [`MoveParty`][osrlib.crawl.commands.MoveParty] from the cell across that side lands the party on the passage's destination. The move makes the usual can't-move checks, then evaluates the passage's gate as `UseStairs` does, and it costs what a step onto the landing cell costs. It reports what `UseStairs` reports for the same destination, with `via="passage"`, and then a [`PartyMovedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.PartyMovedEvent] for the landing cell. [`LevelSpec.passage_at`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.LevelSpec.passage_at] finds a passage by cell and side, and [`LevelSpec.transition_at`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.LevelSpec.transition_at] and `UseStairs` ignore passages. The player view reports a passage's side as `"open"`, and sight, light, and fighting space treat it as the wall it is. [`validate_adventure`][osrlib.crawl.adventure.validate_adventure] refuses a passage whose side isn't a wall. It also refuses a passage whose far cell, the cell one step across its side, is off the grid, in an area, or not walled on its other three sides, and a passage that lands on its own cell or on any passage's far cell. It refuses two passages on the same cell and side, and a trap whose transition is a passage. A new kind value and an optional field are additive within a schema version, so [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] stays 4. No draw sequence or golden file changed.

### Changed

- [`RefereeView`][osrlib.crawl.views.RefereeView] has one typed field per group a save keeps, each built from the session's own model, so a front end reads `view.monsters[0].current_hp` and `view.flags["key"]` with the types this reference documents instead of indexing a dict of raw JSON by hand (#105). The fields are the groups [`session_state`][osrlib.persistence.session_state] writes, minus `master_seed` and `rng_streams`, so `view.model_dump(mode="json")` is that save payload without the two withheld keys, which is what keeps the two layouts from drifting apart. `view.command_log` and `view.event_log` contain the [`Command`][osrlib.crawl.commands.Command] and [`Event`][osrlib.core.events.Event] subclasses that were issued and emitted, each with its own fields, and an entry a save carried as a raw mapping for an event type this library has no class for stays the mapping it arrived as. The counters a save writes under `exploration` are [`ExplorationCounters`][osrlib.crawl.views.ExplorationCounters], named as the session names them. The view is a snapshot: it contains copies of the session's mutable state, so play going on afterwards leaves it as it was, and building one still reads the session and changes nothing. This change touches no persistence path: `session_state` and [`save_game`][osrlib.persistence.save_game] are as they were, and the view moves no `schema_version`.
- The temple charges the party for a healing service rather than the patient (#73, part of #108). [`PurchaseHealing`][osrlib.crawl.commands.PurchaseHealing] draws the fee from the treated member's purse first and then from the other members in marching order, dead members included. Each purse pays in whole gold pieces, as much of what is still owed as its gold covers, so a purse that cannot cover the rest hands over all of its gold and keeps what it is worth below a gold piece, and the last purse charged pays the outstanding remainder alone. What the party can spend is therefore the whole gold pieces in its purses rather than their total worth, and a party whose whole gold pieces fall short is refused with `items.purchase.insufficient_funds` and keeps every coin, because the check runs before any purse is opened. This is what makes `raise_dead` buyable: the patient is a corpse, nothing hands a corpse coin, and the documented salvage flow (game over, `PlaceParty` to town, raise dead) is funded from the party's pooled wealth. [`HealingPurchasedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.HealingPurchasedEvent] carries `payers` and `payments_gp`, which name the purses charged and what each one put in; both default empty, so an older log still parses, and the default English line names the other payers only when the patient could not cover the fee alone. The service names are one exported type, [`HealingService`][osrlib.crawl.commands.HealingService], which both the command field and the `HEALING_SERVICES` price list use, so the offered list and the accepted list cannot drift apart. No draw sequence changed.
- [`validate_adventure`][osrlib.crawl.adventure.validate_adventure] rejects two authorable configurations that save clean and can never fire (#46, #47). An area id colliding with a feature id on the same level is rejected: the two share the trap-reference namespace `"<dungeon>:<level>:<id>"` that gates found, sprung, and removed trap state, so one trap's spring would stand for the other's, and an untouched cache could read as already sprung. An `open`-trigger room trap on an area none of whose boundary edges is a door is rejected too, because opening a door is the only action that springs one and an area with no door edge could never roll for it; a door that `starts_open` still counts, since a referee command or a trigger can close it later. Neither check touches an `enter`-trigger trap, which springs on the party stepping into the area and needs no door at all. An author fixes either shape before the adventure passes validation.
- The found-trap rule is pinned rather than inherited, in the adaptations register and on [`TrapEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.TrapEvent] (#48). A found *room* trap never springs, at its area's edge or at one of its doors, while a found *treasure* trap still rolls its 2-in-6 on every [`TakeTreasure`][osrlib.crawl.commands.TakeTreasure] until a thief removes it, because in B/X anyone can find and avoid a room trap and only a thief can take a treasure trap out. The consequence a front end needs is that `exploration.trap.safe` is a cache outcome only: a door past a found room trap rolls no die and so has nothing to report, and a known-trap-fails-to-fire beat rendered off that code will never fire at a door. Documentation only; no behaviour, event, or draw sequence changed.
- `commands.json` and `events.json`, the downloadable discriminated-union JSON Schemas under the command and event reference, describe every property: each one's `description` comes from its attribute docstring (#103). Every `description` in both files, the model's own and each property's, is the plain name a mkdocstrings cross-reference links rather than the cross-reference syntax, since a schema reaches a reader, or a tool-calling LLM, as text rather than through the site's markdown pipeline; the JSON Schema block on each command's and event's own reference page reads the same way.
- A defensive move is the whole party's or nobody's (#123). [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] refuses a round in which some of the members it expects to declare chose `fighting_withdrawal` or `retreat` and the rest chose something else, with the new code `battle.declaration.formation_split`. The party moves as one formation and a member can't leave it, so a defensive move only part of the party makes is not a legal declaration for that member, and a front end that offers the two moves per member has a refusal to show the player. There is one rejection per defensive move the round split on, and its params name the `move`, the `declared` ids, and the `others`, meaning every other declarer of that round, each tuple in marching order. A round that splits on both moves comes back with two rejections, the fighting withdrawal's first and the retreat's second, and each one names the other move's declarers among its `others`. The refusal happens in the validation pre-phase beside every other declaration rejection, so a split round costs no draw, no round increment, no distance, and no event, and the whole command rejects listing every rejection as it already did. `close` is not a defensive move and needs no agreement: the first `close` in marching order advances the formation whenever the round is accepted, and a `close` declared beside a `fighting_withdrawal` or a `retreat` counts among the `others` that split the round, so that round is refused and nothing advances. A round whose only declarer declares a defensive move is legal, because everyone agreed. An accepted round is unchanged: no draw sequence moved and no golden file moved with it.

### Removed

- [`RefereeView`][osrlib.crawl.views.RefereeView]`.state`, the one dict that held the whole save payload, is gone (#105). Read the group you want off the field of the same name, `view.flags` for `state["flags"]` and `view.dungeon_state` for `state["dungeon_state"]`, and call `view.model_dump(mode="json")` for the payload whole. This is a removal from the public API, as the rest of this section is, so the release it lands in is a major version. No save document changes: a view was never one.
- A battle declaration's `move` is `close`, `fighting_withdrawal`, or `retreat`, and the `withdraw` value, which names no rule in B/X, is gone: a declaration that carries it fails to parse (#74). `Combat.md` prints two ways out of melee and no third, the fighting withdrawal, backwards at up to half the encounter rate with clear space behind, and the retreat, at full rate with no attack that round while the opponent attacks at +2 and ignores the shield, so a front end offering a fall-back choice sends one of those two. Removing a wire value moves the serialization axis: [`SCHEMA_VERSION`][osrlib.versioning.SCHEMA_VERSION] is 4 for this release, and the 3-to-4 step in [`MIGRATIONS`][osrlib.persistence.MIGRATIONS] clears the value off a logged declaration wherever it sits, since schema 3 never cross-validated `move` against `action`: a `move` declaration becomes `action="hold"` with no move, which is what that round played as, and any other action keeps itself and loses a field nothing read. The adaptations register now pins the other half of the SRD's fighting withdrawal as well: RAW takes no attack away from the withdrawing combatant, but a member declares one thing per round and the whole formation moves together, so a round the party withdraws in is a round it does not attack in. No rule, no rejection, and no draw sequence changed.
- [`EncounterGroup`][osrlib.crawl.encounter.EncounterGroup]`.surrendered` is gone, and with it the `battle.side.surrendered` code on [`MonsterFledEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MonsterFledEvent] and its default English template (#100). A monster group is slain or it runs away, so the `outcome` string on [`MonsterDefeatedEvent`][osrlib.crawl.events.MonsterDefeatedEvent] and on [`DefeatedMonsterRecord`][osrlib.crawl.session.DefeatedMonsterRecord] is `"slain"` or `"routed"` and a front end needs no surrender branch. A schema 3 save that carries the key on a group loads with it dropped, because a model ignores a key it does not declare.
- [`Character`][osrlib.core.character.Character]`.literacy` is gone (#100). A character's band comes from [`AbilityTables.literacy`][osrlib.core.abilities.AbilityTables.literacy] given the intelligence score, which is the one statement of the rule. The property never reached a save, so no document changes.
- [`DungeonState`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DungeonState]`.discovered_features` is gone (#100). What the party has found lives where the engine writes it: a secret door on [`DoorState.discovered`][osrlib.crawl.dungeon.DoorState], and a found feature on the dungeon state's `found_traps` and `found_tricks`. A schema 3 save that carries the key loads with it dropped.
- A ward effect definition's `unbreakable` param is gone (#100). Melee against a monster a protection ward barred breaks the ward, and an effect definition has no way to hold it open.
- `osrlib.crawl.exploration.HANDLERS`, `osrlib.crawl.encounter.HANDLERS`, and `osrlib.crawl.battle.HANDLERS` are gone (#107). Each module's command-to-handler table is private now, and the merged table a session dispatches from lives on the session instance, built on its own first dispatch, rather than in a module-level cache every session shared, so replacing an entry after a session's first command reaches only that session. [`GameSession.execute`][osrlib.crawl.session.GameSession.execute] is the one documented way to run a command: a handler pulled straight out of one of these tables skips the mode gate, the command log, the listeners, and the validation pre-phase that `execute` runs around it.

### Fixed

- Discovering a secret door clears the `room_traps` search attempts of both cells of that edge, so every member may search either cell again (#45, #129). A [`Search`][osrlib.crawl.commands.Search] for `room_traps` covers the searched cell's door edges, and an undiscovered secret door hides the trap beyond it along with itself, so a member who searches while the door is still wall spends their attempt with no chance at that trap. The refund fires on a `secret_doors` search that finds the door and on the referee's [`SetDoorState`][osrlib.crawl.commands.SetDoorState] with `discovered=True`, and it clears nothing else: `secret_doors` and `construction` attempts on that cell, and every attempt on every other cell, stand. The refund reaches the far cell too, whichever side the discovery came from, because a search from either cell covers the same edge.
- [`check_fatigue`][osrlib.crawl.exploration.check_fatigue] attaches the unrested-fatigue penalty to each living member who lacks it, so a member who joins an already-tired party, or who loses the effect some other way, is fatigued beside their companions rather than waiting for the next [`Rest`][osrlib.crawl.commands.Rest] to reset the counter (#100). `exploration.fatigue.gained` is emitted when at least one member gained the effect on the call, and the call returns nothing when every living member already carries it, so a fully fatigued party reports the check as quiet.
- A battle declaration is judged again in the magic phase, immediately before it resolves, so a cast or a scroll read the rules can no longer allow fizzles instead of going off or raising out of the round (#129). [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] re-runs every check a `cast` declaration and a scroll-read `use_item` declaration passed when the round was accepted, against the state as it stands then and before anything is spent or drawn, and failing any one of them fizzles the declaration. That is the rule; which check fails is whatever the round did to the caster. Two that happen: an ally's *silence 15' radius* anchors on the party's cell when a creature passes its save, so a silence resolving earlier in the same magic phase silences a caster whose declaration was accepted with no silence on the cell, and the party's last torch goes out as its bearer dies in the missile phase, leaving a scroll reader without the light a read needs. A refused declaration is not cast: the caster loses the memorized copy, or the scroll its spell, exactly as a disruption loses it, and the round reports it with a [`SpellDisruptedEvent`][osrlib.core.events.SpellDisruptedEvent] at the new code `magic.cast.fizzled`, whose new `reason` field holds the first rejection code the re-check produced, `magic.cast.silenced_area` for the silence and `exploration.action.requires_light` for the lost torch. Read `reason` rather than assuming a case, and match on it the way you match on a rejection's own code. `reason` defaults `None`, so a log written before the field existed still parses, and a `magic.cast.disrupted` event leaves it unset: a caster the round already disrupted keeps reporting `magic.cast.disrupted`, because that check runs before the re-check. A scroll read that fizzles emits its `items.scroll.read` event first, because the reader did read it. A declaration the re-check accepts resolves as it always did, with the same draws in the same order.
- A scroll read is judged at the scroll's own caster level, in the kernel and in the crawl layer alike (#117). [`cast_from_scroll`][osrlib.core.spells.cast_from_scroll] validates and resolves at the spell's minimum caster level, so the two things that scale with caster level follow the scroll rather than the reader: a *magic missile* scroll takes one target however high the reader's level, and a per-level range is measured from the scroll's reach. The kernel exports the pre-check itself as [`validate_scroll_cast`][osrlib.core.spells.validate_scroll_cast], which builds the scroll-level caster and returns the rejections `cast_from_scroll` would raise on; `cast_from_scroll` runs it, and so do [`UseItem`][osrlib.crawl.commands.UseItem] and the `use_item` battle declaration, so there is one statement of the rule and a front end deciding whether to offer a read can ask it too. The crawl therefore refuses exactly what the kernel would refuse, before any state change, draw, or event: a 6th-level magic-user reading a *magic missile* scroll is refused `magic.cast.target_count` for three targets and keeps the scroll.
- [`generate_treasure`][osrlib.core.treasure.generate_treasure], [`generate_treasure_entries`][osrlib.core.treasure.generate_treasure_entries], and [`generate_unguarded_treasure`][osrlib.core.treasure.generate_unguarded_treasure] raise `ValueError` for a `tier` that is neither `"basic"` nor `"expert"` before their first draw, the check [`generate_magic_item`][osrlib.core.treasure.generate_magic_item] already makes. A bad tier costs no draws and yields no hoard, so a caller who passes a typo sees the bug rather than a plausible pile of coins and gems. A valid call's draw sequence is unchanged.

## [1.7.0] - 2026-08-22

### Added

- The player's encounter view now states the shape of the round the engine is asking a front end to declare. [`EncounterView`][osrlib.crawl.views.EncounterView] adds four id tuples beside the monster groups — `declarers` (living and able to act, the exact roster a [`ResolveBattleRound`][osrlib.crawl.commands.ResolveBattleRound] must name), `front_rank` (the living members at the current formation width, or every living member with `formation_width_limit` off), `immobile` (declarers who cannot move), and `reloading` (members who fired a `reload` weapon last round, empty unless the `weapon_reload` flag is on). Each one corresponds to exactly one rejection that would otherwise bounce a whole round — `roster_mismatch`, `not_in_front_rank`, `cannot_move`, `combat.attack.reload` — so a front end can offer only the declarations the engine will accept instead of guessing. `front_rank` is the reason the set exists: the width depends on the space the party is standing in, which no wire client can derive, and a front end that assumed "the first two" both denied a legal attack in a room and offered an illegal one in a passage. All four are additive with empty defaults and derived from state a player already sees; no draw sequence and no rejection changed.
- An identified enchanted arm's public combat facts now reach the player view. [`MemberView`][osrlib.crawl.views.MemberView]'s masked inventory adds `qualities` and `missile_ranges` to an identified magic item that resolves to a base weapon — a shield or a suit of armour gains neither field, and an unidentified item still shows only its category display name. Both fields are rulebook facts about a weapon the player has already identified, and without them an enchanted dagger was unclassifiable where a plain dagger was not: a front end could not tell whether declaring the enchanted one at 5 feet meant a throw or a swing.

### Changed

- Formation width is now measured from the space the party stands in, rather than a flat number per kind of cell. The old rule gave three abreast inside any keyed area and two in a corridor; the SRD prints neither number for a room. What it prints is one data point and an instruction: "The referee should judge the number of opponents that can attack a single combatant, bearing in mind the combatant's size and the available space around them. **10' passage:** Enough space for at most 2–3 characters to fight side-by-side." osrlib keeps its pin of the conservative end — two in ten feet, five feet of frontage each — and applies it to the widest square of unbroken floor that includes the party's cell. A one-cell passage holds two however far it runs, and holds two at a junction where two such passages cross; a room two cells square holds four; a room four cells across holds eight. The flood measuring that square stops at walls, at doors (a doorway is a threshold, not room to fight abreast), and where the space itself changes, so a room's open mouth onto a corridor never counts the corridor as part of the room. `formation_width_limit` still switches the whole cap off. The width bounds more than who may swing — the monsters' melee pool is the party's front rank too, a group's reachable rank is its first `width` living members, and an area effect's footprint is `ceil(span / 10) × width` — so rooms are now more dangerous in both directions, which is the point: a room that lets four of yours fight lets four of theirs fight back. `FIGHTER_FRONTAGE_FEET` is exported for front ends that show the player why. The phase 4 and phase 5 scenario goldens move with it, and the milestone script's coin hand-off names a different recipient: the wider rank in the vault exposed the cleric, who now dies there, and the TUI walkthrough's captured transcripts are recaptured from the real run.

## [1.5.0] - 2026-08-07

### Added

- Authored quests, the state they advance, and the ending they reach. A `QuestSpec` (`osrlib.crawl.quests`) is adventure data like a trigger: an optional activation clause, `ObjectiveSpec`s each with a completion clause of their own, `rewards` from the same `ConsequenceCommand` surface under the same `@party`/`@first` selectors, a completion rule of `"all"` or `"any"`, and an optional `concludes_adventure` marker. Every "did this happen?" is a `TriggerClause` — a `TriggerPattern` plus the conditions gates use, matched by the same matcher and the same live evaluation triggers get, so the two surfaces cannot drift; a consuming condition is rejected at parse, because a quest observes and does not take. `Adventure.quests` carries them in document order, with an `Adventure.quest(id)` accessor, and `validate_adventure` walks every clause and reward through the same reference and party-selector checks a trigger gets. Quest state is engine-owned: `GameSession.quests` maps each quest id to a `QuestState` (`inactive` → `active` → `completed`) holding an `ObjectiveState` (`revealed`, `complete`) per objective, seeded at construction from the adventure — activation-less quests active from round 0, objectives visible unless authored `hidden` — so `new`, `load_game`, and replay all begin from the same block. Four referee commands are its only writers: `ActivateQuest`, `RevealObjective`, `CompleteObjective` (which reveals as it completes), and `CompleteQuest`, each emitting a player-visible event carrying the authored beat — `QuestActivatedEvent`, `ObjectiveRevealedEvent`, `ObjectiveCompletedEvent`, `QuestCompletedEvent`, and, when a concluding quest finishes outside a terminal mode, `AdventureCompletedEvent` as the session clears any open encounter or battle and enters `victory`. Unlike `MarkTriggerFired`'s open trigger id, their ids are a closed domain resolved against the adventure's own specs (`session.command.unknown_quest`, `session.command.unknown_objective`), and a command contradicting the state it finds rejects with `session.command.quest_state`; `CompleteQuest` deliberately checks only that the quest is active, because ruling a quest done is the referee's call. Each beat appends its display text to the journal as itself, with no `JournalEntryAddedEvent` behind it — the lifecycle event *is* that beat's event. The `Interpreter` plays all of it: per event, the adventure's triggers in document order and then its quests, issuing every lifecycle command, reward, and note stamped `source="quest:{id}"`, checking the completion rule the moment a completion it issued lands, and dropping a reward that cannot land — a spawn in `victory`, say — with a note rather than a raise. The block persists under a new `quests` key with no `schema_version` bump: a save written without it keeps the constructor's seed, and an adventure that authors no quests plays exactly as before.
- `PlayerView.quests` — the errands the party is on, as `QuestView`s: id, name, the offer beat, the narrative block's `speaker` attribution (a wire client holds no adventure document to resolve one from), and the revealed objectives as `ObjectiveView`s with `"incomplete"` or `"complete"` states. Active quests only, in document order: a quest nobody has been given is absent, and a finished one leaves the list, its record standing in the journal. Hidden objectives have no view at all until something surfaces them, and no clause, pattern, condition, reward, or guidance ever crosses — quest wiring is the game's secret exactly as trigger wiring is.
- `LevelSpec.guidance` — one ambient steering slot per dungeon level, for the tone of a place that hangs on no mechanical object. Inert authored data: the engine reads it nowhere, no event carries it, and it applies while the party occupies the level. A narrating front end reaches it through the adventure document, which is referee-side by construction, since the player view ships no level internals.
- Authored triggers, and the library-shipped listener that plays them. A `TriggerSpec` (`osrlib.crawl.triggers`) binds an observable event pattern — `AreaEnteredPattern`, `LevelEnteredPattern`, `DungeonEnteredPattern`, `TownEnteredPattern`, `ItemAcquiredPattern`, `MonsterDefeatedPattern`, `FlagSetPattern`, a discriminated union that grows additively — optionally narrowed by the same conditions gates use, to referee-command consequences; `Adventure.triggers` carries them, and the tuple's order is document order. A game plays them by registering an `Interpreter` (`osrlib.crawl.interpreter`) on its session, once, the same way it registers any listener. A firing issues `MarkTriggerFired` first, carrying the narrative block's `fired` beat, then the consequences in authored order, then `AddJournalEntry` when the block carries a journal form — every command stamped `source="trigger:{id}"`, so the log answers *why* on its own. Triggers are once-only unless `repeatable`, and the fired-mark is session state, so once-only survives a save, a load, and a replay. The consequence surface is the new `ConsequenceCommand` union over `CONSEQUENCE_COMMAND_CLASSES` — `GrantItem`, `GrantCoins`, `AwardXP`, `SetFlag`, `SpawnMonsters`, `SpawnNpcParty`, `SetDoorState`, `PlaceParty`, `AdvanceTime` — so a document naming a lifecycle command, a player command, or an unknown type fails to parse; a consequence addressing a character uses the party selectors `PARTY_SELECTOR` (`"@party"`, expanded at issue time to one command per living member in marching order) or `FIRST_LIVING_SELECTOR` (`"@first"`, the lead survivor), because character ids are allocated per session and no document can know one. Nothing about a firing is all-or-nothing: a rejected consequence is dropped by itself while the rest still run, and a `RecordNote` names the trigger, the consequence's position and type, and the rejection code. Cascades are bounded — a trigger's events are one level deeper than the event that fired it, matching stops below depth five, and a suppressed firing is recorded as a note rather than a mark, so a once-only trigger cut short there is still fireable later. `validate_adventure` resolves every trigger reference (pattern areas, levels, dungeons, items, and monsters; condition items; consequence items, monsters, doors, and placements) and rejects a literal character id. The interpreter emits no events and keeps no state — its `listener_state` slot stays empty for the life of the session — so a replay, which runs with no listeners at all, rebuilds the same world from the same log. All of it is additive: no schema bump, no migration, and an adventure that authors no triggers plays exactly as before.
- `MarkTriggerFired.narrative` and `TriggerFiredEvent.narrative` — the authored beat for a firing, carried at referee visibility, which `format_message` appends verbatim after the templated line. Trigger wiring is the game's secret, so the `fired` beat is the referee's line; the players' line for the same moment is the trigger's journal form, which rides the player-visible journal event and the player view.
- `LocationEnteredEvent.dungeon_id` — populated on area entries, where it was the missing fact: area ids are scoped to their level, while level and dungeon entries already name the dungeon in `location_id` and town has neither. An area crossing is now self-describing, so a consumer never has to ask the session where the party is standing to know where the event happened.
- `flag_values_equal` (`osrlib.crawl.gates`) — the one strict flag comparison, equality plus matching boolness, so a stored `True` never satisfies an authored `1` in either direction. `FlagEqualsCondition` and the authored flag pattern both compare through it and can never disagree about what equality means.
- The lifecycle command surface an authored trigger or quest layer writes its bookkeeping with, plus the annotation that says on whose behalf a command was issued. Every `Command` gains an optional `source` — a string naming the authored object (a trigger or quest id) or the game system that issued it, never the empty string. Execution never reads it, so a stamped command does exactly what the unstamped one does; it rides the command into the log and survives a save, a load, and a replay, which is what makes "why did the party get that item?" answerable from the log alone. Three new referee commands, legal in every mode (terminal ones included) and rejecting nothing: `MarkTriggerFired` appends a trigger id to the new `session.fired_triggers`, the state that answers once-only semantics — marking an already-marked trigger is accepted, appends nothing, and still emits its referee-visibility `TriggerFiredEvent` (`session.trigger.fired`), so state records that a trigger has fired while the log records each firing; `AddJournalEntry` appends a `JournalEntry` — the authored text plus the clock position it landed at — to the new `session.journal` and emits the player-visible `JournalEntryAddedEvent` (`session.journal.entry_added`) carrying the whole entry; and `RecordNote` records an annotation with no state effect at all, emitting the referee-visibility `NoteRecordedEvent` (`session.note.recorded`) — the mechanism for machine-issued records and a referee's own margin notes alike. `PlayerView` gains `journal`, the entries shipped verbatim; the fired-marks and the notes stay referee-only, since content wiring is the game's secret. Both blocks persist under new payload keys with empty defaults, so there is no schema bump and no migration: a save written before them loads with both empty and starts remembering, and because these commands are the blocks' only writers, a replay — which runs with no listeners registered — rebuilds them exactly by re-executing the log. Nothing in the library issues these commands yet; they are the documented referee surface, and the library-shipped interpreter that drives them arrives with the authored trigger and quest layer.
- `SessionMode.VICTORY` — the second terminal mode, the session that ended by finishing what it set out to do, beside `game_over`'s ending by wipe. Both answer the new `SessionMode.terminal` property, the one place "has this session ended?" is decided, for the engine and for a front end's loop alike. The legality contract in a terminal mode: every play command is illegal (`session.command.wrong_mode`, its `mode` param carrying `victory`), and every referee command is legal — grants, awards, flags, door writes, identification, time, dice — which is what lets an adventure's rewards land after it concludes. Three referee commands are the exception, each because it would resume play in a session that is over: `SpawnMonsters` and `SpawnNpcParty` open an encounter and are illegal in both terminal modes, and `PlaceParty` teleports the party into a play mode and is illegal in `victory`. `PlaceParty` remains legal in `game_over`, where it is the salvage door — `PlaceParty(town)` then `PurchaseHealing(service="raise_dead")`, with the clock still running on the revival window. Nothing in the library transitions *into* `victory` yet; the entrance arrives with the authored quest layer, and the mode, its property, and its legality rules ship first so that transition has a contract to land on. The new enum value is additive within the current `schema_version`: a `victory` save is one an older engine has never seen, the documented accepted risk for a new serialized enum value, and there is no migration.
- Authored gates on doors and level transitions: `DoorSpec.requires` and `TransitionSpec.requires` carry a `GateSpec` — a condition the party must satisfy for opening the door or taking the stair to be a legal command. The condition vocabulary is the new discriminated union in `osrlib.crawl.gates`: `HasItemCondition` (some member's carried inventory holds an item with that catalog id — equipment or magic item, equipped slots included — optionally `consumes=True`), `FlagEqualsCondition` (a session flag holds a value, compared strictly: an absent key matches nothing, not even `False`, and a stored `True` never satisfies an authored `1`), and `EffectActiveCondition` (an active effect of that kind is attached to a party member). Evaluation is pure and level-triggered — `condition_holds` reads live state at the moment of the attempt and stores nothing, so a key dropped or sold stops opening its door — and the member domain is the whole party, living or dead, because the party carries its dead and their packs. A failed gate is an ordinary rejection with its own codes, `exploration.door.gate_refused` and `exploration.transition.gate_refused`, each carrying the author's refusal text when one was written; it is checked last, after every mundane refusal, so it fires exactly when the gate alone bars the way, and on `ForceDoor` that means a refused attempt makes no noise, denies no surprise, and rolls no die. Gates and locks are orthogonal layers: a door carrying both requires both, `PickLock` addresses only the lock, `SetDoorState` rewrites only the overlay, and a door standing open admits passage unchecked until it closes again. `validate_adventure` resolves `has_item` ids against the effective equipment catalog or the magic-item catalog; flag keys and effect kinds are open domains and stay unchecked. The fields are additive with `None` defaults, so there is no schema bump and no migration: existing documents and saves load unchanged, and an ungated adventure plays exactly as before, consuming no extra draws.
- `NarrativeBlock` (`osrlib.crawl.narrative`) — the authored text attached to a mechanical object, in three audiences: display beats a deterministic renderer shows verbatim (`refusal` and `success` for gates; `fired` for triggers; `offer`, `progress`, and `completion` for quests), a `journal` form, and `guidance` for an LLM narrator that is never displayed as written, plus a free-prose `speaker` attribution. Gates read the first two: `refusal` rides the rejection, and `success` rides the successful command's own event through the new optional `DoorEvent.narrative` and `LocationEnteredEvent.narrative` fields, which `format_message` appends verbatim after the templated line. Authored text on an event is content data in a structured field — events still carry message codes and never engine-baked English.
- `Inventory.carried_item` — the one answer to "does this pack hold item X": the first carried instance of a catalog id, mundane (matched on its template's id) or magic (matched on its `template_id`), across the whole carried surface — pack, hands, worn slots, rings — in carried order. Valuables never match, since gems and jewellery carry no catalog id, and a spent stack (quantity zero, expressible only on a magic instance such as an emptied quiver of arrows +1) is not carrying, so anything that finds an instance this way can also take a unit from it. A `has_item` gate, the consumption primitive behind every toll and every spike, and the item hand-off paths all evaluate through this one method rather than each carrying its own notion of "carried".
- `ItemConsumedEvent` (`exploration.item.consumed`, player visibility) — one carried item was used up. A `consumes=True` gate takes its toll from the first holder in marching order when the gated command succeeds, and the event lands before the command's own: the toll is paid at the threshold. Consumption is per success, so a toll door charges again every time it is opened. A mundane consumption reports the catalog id; a magic one reports the instance's session-scoped `instance_id` and never its `template_id`, the masking rule `ItemAcquiredEvent` already follows.
- `Adventure.items` — an adventure document can bundle its own `WeaponTemplate`, `ArmourTemplate`, `GearTemplate`, and `AmmunitionTemplate`s, the same models the shipped equipment lists are made of, which join the shipped catalog for that adventure's sessions everywhere the engine resolves an authored item id: a treasure cache's `item_ids`, `GrantItem`, and the drop-pile recovery behind `TakeTreasure`. This is the item-identity surface for authored objects — the quest key, the temple idol — which previously had to be an id-less generic valuable and so could never be named by a cache, a grant, or anything observing what the party carries. Downstream of acquisition nothing differs: an `ItemInstance` embeds its whole template, so equipping, encumbrance, combat, `GiveItems`, drops, persistence, and replay carry bundled items unmodified, and `ItemAcquiredEvent` reports their ids exactly as it reports shipped ones. Bundled ids must not collide with the equipment catalog, the magic-item catalog, or each other — one item id names one thing per session; collisions fail `validate_adventure` (and, for doctored saves, `load_game`) with `ContentValidationError`, never silently overriding a shipped item. Treasure-weight ids (`coin`, `gem`, …) are an encumbrance table rather than item identity and stay outside the rule. The session exposes the union as the read-only `GameSession.effective_equipment` property. One deliberate boundary: the town shop stocks the shipped equipment lists, so `PurchaseEquipment` naming a bundled item rejects with the new `items.purchase.not_stocked` code rather than selling a second copy of the idol from room 12 — distinct from `session.command.unknown_item`, which still means no such id exists at all. The field is additive with an empty default, so there is no schema bump: existing documents and saves load unchanged, and an adventure that bundles nothing plays exactly as before.

- `FeatureSpec.magic_item_ids` — hand-placed magic items in authored treasure caches. A cache could always carry coins, mundane equipment ids, and named valuables, but a specific magic item — the sword +1 in the captain's strongbox — was unplaceable: magic entered play only through generated hoards, so an author wanting a particular item in a particular chest had no surface to say so. The new field names any id from the shipped magic-item catalog (see the magic item id index); the instance is created when the cache is emptied, its creation details (charges, quantities, generic armour's base type, scroll contents, sword sentience) rolling on the treasure stream at the tier in force at that moment, exactly as a generated hoard's would — authored content stays frozen, and a save made before the take carries no undiscoverable referee facts. Listing an id twice places two. The instantiation half of `generate_magic_item` is now its own public function, `instantiate_magic_item`, for any caller that needs a named item's details rolled without the table selection. `validate_adventure` resolves the new ids against the magic-item catalog (an unknown id is a hard content error before play), and `validate_content_pack` reports the same gap as the new `pack.feature.unknown_magic_item` finding. The field is additive with an empty default, so there is no schema bump: existing documents load unchanged, and a cache that places no magic items plays exactly as before, consuming no extra draws.

- `CharacterLeveledUpEvent` (`session.level.gained`, player visibility) — fires immediately after a member's `XpAwardedEvent` whenever an XP award crosses a level threshold, on every award surface: the end-of-adventure award, the immediate timing, and the referee's `AwardXP`. It carries the levels before and after, the hit points gained, the raw hit die roll (`None` past name level, where the gain is the flat-bonus delta), whether the CON modifier applied, and the class's level title at the new level (`None` past the printed title list). Previously a level gain surfaced only as the `level_after` field on the XP award, so a front end had no event to announce the moment on — and no hit-point or title facts to announce it with.
- `LearnSpell`, a command that adds a spell to an arcane caster's spell book in-session — leveling's new capacity or a mentor's teaching made concrete, driving the core `add_spell_to_book` through the command loop so the addition is validated, logged, and replayed like any other command. Legal in town and while exploring, and no game time passes: the fiction around the learning (the mentor's week, a copied scroll's costs) belongs to the game. The per-spell-level capacity, the duplicate/wrong-list/non-arcane gates, and the book-never-shrinks rule are exactly the core function's, and a successful learning emits the existing `SpellBookUpdatedEvent`.
- `open_book_capacity` — the per-spell-level count of open spell-book slots: the progression row's capacity minus the spells held at that level, floored at zero (a drained caster's book may sit over capacity and simply reads as no openings), `()` for a class with no arcane book. `add_spell_to_book`'s capacity check now consults it, so the rule has one home a front end can share when rendering what a caster may still learn. Alongside it, `level_title` answers a class's level title at a given level, `None` beyond the printed list — the level event reads it.

### Changed

- The example TUI crawler renders from the result envelope and shows the authored layer whole. Its loop now iterates `result.events` instead of diffing `session.event_log` — the envelope already carries everything a nested listener-issued command logged, so the delta idiom's stated rationale was false — and a rejection now prints the authored `refusal` text after its code when a gate wrote one, so the one rejection family carrying player-facing words stops being silently dropped by the renderer everyone copies. `status` lists the active quests with their revealed objectives' states from `PlayerView.quests`, and a new `journal` verb prints `PlayerView.journal` with its clock stamps — a pure view read, executing no command and drawing nothing; the milestone script gains one `journal` line before its closing `status`. No command sequence or draw changed, and the scenario goldens stand byte-for-byte.
- Both example front ends now author their fetch quest as adventure data and register the library's `Interpreter` to play it; the hand-rolled `FetchQuestListener` is deleted with no shim. The Jade Idol became a bundled `GearTemplate` placed in the shrine cache by id, so taking it reports a catalog id an `ItemAcquiredPattern` matches and a `has_item` condition tests, and the homecoming objective is a `TownEnteredPattern` narrowed by that condition — walking back empty-handed is not a return. The quest concludes the adventure, so the milestone transcript restructured into two trips: the delve and the town business first, because selling and healing are illegal once the session is in `victory`, then back down for the idol and home to the completion, the rewards, and the ending — the TUI gaining a `give` verb along the way, because a sold haul is a purse full of coin and coin weighs a coin apiece. Its economics moved with it — the idol is mundane gear now, worth no treasure XP by RAW, and the reward lands in town after the last award has fired — so the authored `AwardXP` rose from 600 to 1200 per member, which restores the run's XP totals exactly. The example listener was the extension-surface proof; the guides keep teaching that pattern with a self-contained listener of their own, and the interpreter as the shipped instance of it.
- A `CommandResult` now carries the events of commands a listener issued while reacting. A listener that reacts by executing further commands has always logged their events correctly and reported none of them back: the caller of `MoveParty` got the move and nothing of the portcullis that opened in response, and had to read `session.event_log` to find the rest. `execute` now notes where the log ends before each listener runs and folds everything logged while it ran into the result — the nested commands' events, however deeply they nest, each exactly once and in log order, followed by whatever the listener authored. A listener that emits events and issues no commands is unaffected, and nothing about what reaches the log changes.
- `SpawnMonsters` and `SpawnNpcParty` no longer execute in `game_over`. Every referee command used to be legal in every mode without exception, which meant a referee could spawn a wandering patrol onto a party that had already fallen — and the encounter that opened put a concluded session back into `encounter`, or straight into `battle` on an attacking reaction, with corpses on one side of it. Spawning was never part of the salvage flow (that door is `PlaceParty`, and a session salvaged back to town can spawn again the moment it re-enters a dungeon), so both commands now reject with `session.command.wrong_mode` in `game_over` as well as in the new `victory`. Every other referee command still runs in a terminal mode.
- `WedgeDoor` now reports the iron spike it consumes. Wedging a door has always taken a spike out of a member's pack, silently: nothing in the event stream said so, and a front end had to diff inventories to notice. It now emits `ItemConsumedEvent` before the `exploration.door.wedged` event, the same way a gate's toll reports itself, so there is one consumption surface rather than a speaking one and a silent one. The phase 4 and phase 5 scenario goldens moved to record the added event and the new optional `narrative` field on the door and location events; no draw sequence changed.

### Fixed

- Naming one magic item twice in a single `GiveItems` or `DropItems` now rejects instead of raising. A magic instance id names exactly one instance, and the whole instance leaves its owner on the first naming — but validation counted only mundane ids, so the second naming passed the pre-phase and then found nothing: `GiveItems` raised a `ValueError` out of `execute`, breaking the contract that a schema-valid command rejects rather than throws, and `DropItems` silently half-ran. Both now answer `exploration.item.not_carried` in the validation pre-phase, mutating nothing.
- Sight persistence now runs before the listeners, so a listener that relocates the party leaves a seen map a replay rebuilds exactly. The fold of the party's current light reveal into map memory happened after every accepted command *and after its listeners*, so a listener whose reaction teleported the party folded the destination's view in place of the view from where the party actually was — while a replay, executing the same commands with no listeners, folded both in order. Live and replay now fold identically. No stored golden changed: the goldens record state and logs, and the event log was already interleaved correctly.
- A party wiped out by anything other than a lost battle now ends the session. Only battle routed to `game_over`: a party that died to a save-or-die trap, a fall, poison, or starvation was left in `exploring` (or `town`, or mid-`encounter`) forever — every play command still nominally legal with nobody alive to issue it, no `GameOverEvent`, and no way for a front end to know the game was over. Every wipe now ends the session identically, wherever the killing blow came from and whichever mode the session was in, with the one `GameOverEvent` a lost battle has always emitted; battle's own defeat branch no longer transitions, so the ending is constructed in exactly one place and the event stream a lost battle produces is unchanged. The check is triggered by a death, not by the state of the party, so the documented salvage flow still works: `PlaceParty(town)` carrying an already-fallen party out of `game_over` does not fall straight back into it. A session already in a terminal mode never transitions again — a death after the adventure has concluded leaves `victory` alone. Alongside it, the procedures that ran on after a mid-command wipe now stop: a chute that kills the party on the way down still moves the bodies and still costs its time, but no longer discovers the destination's treasure or opens its keyed encounter; an encounter round, a pursuit round, or a turning attempt whose round kills the last member resolves no stance action, no reaction re-roll, no distraction die, and starts no battle among corpses; a treasure trap that kills everyone who reached for the cache still costs the turn but no longer loots it — the authored valuables and magic items go uninstantiated, no draw is spent on them, and the cache keeps its contents for whoever comes back; and a rest or other in-field span truncates at the wipe rather than running its remaining turns of fatigue and wandering checks. Referee time is untouched — a span outside the field runs in full, because the revival window `raise_dead` reads is measured in elapsed time. Every one of these paths is reachable only once the party is dead, so no living party's draw sequence moves and no golden changed.
- A refused command no longer writes a door into the state overlay. `DungeonState.door(ref)` creates on first touch, and every door handler reached it while *validating*, so a refused `OpenDoor` — locked, stuck, no door there — and a move blocked by a shut door each stored a default door entry: a command that by contract mutates nothing, mutating state. The consequences were real if quiet: a save taken after a refusal diverged from the same save taken after its replay, and the swing-shut pass iterates that map on an event-emitting path, so probing order could reorder events. Door reads now answer a transient default seeded from the authored spec — identical to what a write would have stored — and only the mutations themselves (open, close, force, wedge, unlock, secret-door discovery, and the referee's `SetDoorState`) create the entry. New saves stop accumulating entries for doors nobody ever touched; older saves load unchanged, since a redundant default entry is harmless.
- A referee `SetDoorState` that sets nothing no longer writes a door into the state overlay. The command's four state fields are all optional, and one with none of them set is legal, emits nothing, and changed nothing — yet it still created the door's overlay entry, the same accumulation the read-path fix above removes.
- A referee door write no longer shuts a door the adventure authored open. `SetDoorState` created its overlay entry without the authored `starts_open` seed, so the first referee write to an authored-open door — wedging it, marking it discovered — silently stored it closed. It now writes through the same seeded materializer the play handlers use.
- A room trap authored with `trigger="open"` now springs. The model always accepted `open` on a `kind="room"` trap, but the engine's only room-trap path ran on entering the area and skipped every other trigger value, so the standard B/X construction — the blade that drops when the door swings, the poisoned lock — could be authored, saved, and validated clean, and then never fire at the table (#35). Opening a door is now the springing action: `OpenDoor` and a successful `ForceDoor` give an unresolved `open`-trigger trap on an adjoining area its 2-in-6 spring check, on every opening until the trap springs once. A door belongs to the trapped area from either side — the blade falls on the way out as well as the way in — with the far side rolling first in the corner case of a door joining two trapped areas. Each spring lands on the character who opened the door: the forcing character for `ForceDoor`, otherwise the first living member in marching order, the enter path's hands-on default — and passes to the next member standing if an earlier spring killed the opener. A trap needs a living victim, so a party with no one left rolls no spring die at all, and a spring whose effect carries the party away from the door (a chute) cancels the springs behind it: the party is no longer performing the opening. The find/avoid loop works from the threatened side too: a `room_traps` search now covers the searched cell's door edges, so the trap in the area beyond a known door is findable before the door is ever opened — an undiscovered secret door still hides its trap along with itself, and a found door trap never springs, exactly as a found pit stops springing on movement. The mirror-image dead value is closed at the model instead: `TrapSpec` now rejects `trigger="enter"` on a `kind="treasure"` trap, which the cache path never read — a cache has nothing to enter, and osr-editor already stopped offering the combination. Because that narrows what a serialized document may carry, it ships as **schema version 3**: a pre-3 save — saves embed the full adventure — loads through a migration that rewrites the never-read trigger to `"open"` losslessly, and a pre-3 content pack gets the same repair on load, so no existing document is ever bricked by the new rule. No shipped or fixture content authored either dead combination, the new check draws only when an `open`-trigger trap is actually adjacent, and the enter path is untouched, so draw sequences and goldens are unchanged.
- A save-or-die trap no longer kills a character who passed the saving throw when the save's `on_save` is `"half"`. `_resolve_trap` short-circuited a passed save only when it negated: a passing `half` save set the halved flag — meaningful only to the damage roll, a line the kill branch never reaches — and fell straight through to the kill, so a trap authored as "save versus death, half on a success" played as "everyone the trap catches dies". A passed save now spares the victim from a kill outright, whatever `on_save` says, which is the rule the spell kill path (`_resolve_kill`) has applied all along and the only reading the SRD supports: every half-on-save in the corpus attaches to damage dice (*fire ball*'s "half damage") and every save-or-die form is binary ("save versus poison or die"), so `on_save` scales damage only and half of a kill isn't a thing B/X expresses. The same defect shape held at the trap's other component sites, and the rule now holds at all of them: a passed save likewise spares the victim from the `condition` (the `_resolve_attachment` rule on the spell side — half of a blindness isn't a thing either), and a passed `half` save halves falling damage exactly as it halves rolled damage, where previously the halved flag was read only at the rolled-damage site and a pit authored with a `half` save spared nobody and halved nothing. The `TrapEffect` docstring now states the whole interaction. Draw sequences are effectively untouched — the save rolled before the fix, a kill consumes no draws, and halving applies after the roll; the one draw a spared victim now skips is the attach-time duration roll of a half-save condition trap, a form no shipped data or fixture authors — so goldens are unchanged and an affected document simply stops harming its passers.
- What the party has seen by its own light now persists as map memory. The 1.2.1 reveal was deliberately ephemeral: the player view computed the lit room and passages fresh on every build, so the moment the party walked on — or the torch guttered out — every cell it had glimpsed but not stepped on fell dark again, and a front end's automap could remember only the physically walked footprint. Seen cells now accumulate in `DungeonState.seen`, a second per-level cell list beside `explored`, written by the session after every accepted command from the same light flood the view draws; the player projection carries walked plus remembered-seen plus currently lit cells, so a mapped room stays on the map. Seen is memory of sight, never exploration: movement cost still reads the walked footprint (a lit-but-unwalked cell costs the full 30), drop-pile visibility stays on explored cells, an undiscovered secret door's sealed alcove is never recorded, and the referee view is unchanged. The field is additive with a default, so there is no schema bump and no migration — an older save loads with empty memory and simply starts remembering.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-07-28

### Added

- `osrlib.crawl.content_pack` — content packs, the portable form of finished keyed content. A `ContentPack` holds sections of geometry-free entries (`AreaSpec` minus its cells: prose, keyed encounter, room trap, treasure declaration, features), each section optionally carrying a level's `WanderingSpec`, plus the bundled `MonsterTemplate`s the entries' encounters and the sections' wandering tables reference — the pack's closure. Section ids, pack-wide entry ids, and bundled monster ids are unique by construction; dangling references stay legal, and `validate_content_pack` reports them as structured `PackFinding`s (dotted snake_case codes, the `Rejection` discipline) instead of raising. Packs serialize as stamped `"content_pack"` documents via `ContentPack.to_document`/`from_document` with the documented acceptance rules: older schema versions load, newer ones fail with `SaveVersionError`, and any write re-stamps at current versions.
- `TakeTreasure.recipient_id` (optional) — names the single party member who fills their pack from the cache or pile, in place of the default spread. The named member is also the one who reaches in, so they are the character an unresolved treasure trap springs on. Goods beyond that member's own maximum load stay on the cell.
- `ItemsLeftBehindEvent` (`exploration.item.left_behind`, player visibility) — reports the goods and coin a haul left in the drop pile on the party's cell because the carriers had no capacity for them.

### Fixed

- A recovered haul now spreads across the living party instead of landing entirely on the first member in marching order. The old behavior was not merely unfair, it was party-stopping: one pickup of 600 sp and 900 cp on an already-laden character crossed the SRD's 1,600-coin maximum load, dropped that character's movement rate to 0, and — since "the movement rate of the party as a whole is determined by the speed of the slowest member" — froze the entire party until a player unwound it by hand with `GiveItems`. `TakeTreasure` now assigns items to a character whose class may use them (the same class policies `validate_equip` enforces, plus a magic item's own `usable_by`, ignoring equipped state), divides gems and jewellery by worth rather than by count, and divides coins evenly denomination by denomination — every coin weighing 1, an even split of a denomination is even in wealth and in weight at once. Nothing is ever loaded past the maximum load, so a pickup cannot immobilise a carrier that some other arrangement would have kept moving; whatever the party cannot carry stays in the cell's drop pile with an `ItemsLeftBehindEvent` rather than being destroyed, goods packing best-first so a party out of capacity keeps the platinum and leaves the copper. The pass is deterministic and spends no RNG draw — ties break on marching order — and the XP award is unchanged, since `party_valuation_cp` sums the whole party however the goods are split. `TakeTreasure` now emits one `ItemAcquiredEvent` per member who took something, which moved the Phase 4 and Phase 5 scenario goldens.
- Encounter distance is now bounded by the space the encounter happens in. The 2d6 × 10' dungeon roll ignored the party's surroundings entirely, so an encounter keyed to a 20' room could open at 120' — six times the room's width — and a rolled distance routinely exceeded the whole dungeon level. The SRD rolls only "if there is uncertainty", since "the situation in which the encounter occurs often determines how far away the monster is"; the walls around the party's cell are that situation, so a rolled distance now caps at the longest unobstructed straight sight line from that cell across the four grid directions, floored at one cell (10'). A corridor still affords its full sight line and the printed 20'–120' band; a room affords no more than the room; shut and undiscovered-secret doors stop the line where they stop the party. Darkness never shortens it — light governs surprise, not distance — and a caller-supplied `distance_feet` (every referee spawn: `SpawnMonsters`, `SpawnNpcParty`) is never capped. The cap reads the rolled result and never the RNG stream, so no draw sequence changes; rolled distances in cramped spaces do change, which moved the Phase 4 and Phase 5 scenario goldens.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-07-24

### Added

- `osrlib.crawl.stocking.stock_area` — the SRD dungeon-stocking procedure that consumes the shipped stocking tables. Given a dungeon level, an effective monster catalog, and one `RngStream`, it rolls a single keyed area's contents (the stocking d6, then the treasure d6 when the row calls for it, then, on a monster room, the encounter table's d20 row, its count, and the variant or per-individual pool picks) and answers a frozen `StockedArea` — content models an author can review, place, and edit. A monster room's rolled individuals group by template into `KeyedMonster` lines with concrete counts; an empty or trap room that rolls treasure gets an unguarded `AreaTreasureSpec`; an NPC-party row reports its rolled kind and count as a `StockedNpcParty` and stops (a party has no keyed content model). Every draw comes from the passed stream in a fixed order, so a stocked area is reproducible from the stream's state alone. Traps and specials produce no models — the procedure ends where the referee's design begins.
- `KeyedEncounter.hoard` (default `True`) — gates whether the engine generates the keyed monsters' lair hoard when the encounter first spawns. The default preserves every existing document's play semantics; `hoard=False` expresses the treasure-absent keyed room (a monster room the SRD stocking roll gave no treasure), which an unconditional lair hoard could not otherwise represent.
- `GiveItems`, a command that hands items and coins from one party member to another in zero game time — the distribute-the-load move for shifting weight off an overloaded companion so the party's marching rate recovers. Legal in town and while exploring (not mid-encounter or in battle); both members must be able-bodied and distinct. A given magic item releases its worn effects and lands unequipped in the recipient's pack, mundane items merge into a like stack, valuables and coins move across, and the transfer emits a player-visible `ItemsGivenEvent`. The giver must actually carry what's named, and a revealed cursed item cannot be handed off.

## [1.2.1] - 2026-07-20

### Fixed

- The player view's explored cells now include what the party sees by its own light from its current cell — the lit room it stands in and open passages out to the light source's radius (a torch's 30 feet, the *light* spell's 15) — rather than only the cells it has physically entered. A front end drawing `PlayerView.explored` renders the torchlit room at once instead of leaving it dark until the party steps onto each square. The reveal is sight, not exploration: it never enters the persisted explored set (so movement cost and map memory are unchanged), it stops at walls and shut or undiscovered doors, and it never reaches the referee view.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-07-17

### Added

- `Adventure.monsters` — an adventure document can bundle its own custom `MonsterTemplate`s, which join the shipped catalog for that adventure's sessions everywhere the engine resolves template ids: keyed encounters, `SpawnMonsters`, inline wandering tables, listen checks, and `GameSession.spawn`. Downstream of spawn nothing changes — combat, XP, treasure, persistence, and replay carry bundled monsters unmodified. Bundled ids must not collide with the shipped catalog or each other; collisions fail `validate_adventure` (and, for doctored saves, `load_game`) with `ContentValidationError`. The session exposes the union as the read-only `GameSession.effective_monsters` property.

### Changed

- `validate_adventure` now checks inline wandering-table monster ids: an adventure whose level wandering table names a dangling monster id — previously accepted by the gate and left to crash at play time — fails validation up front.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-07-05

### Added

- `RollDice`, an authorial command that rolls an arbitrary dice expression through the seeded session for freeform referee adjudication. It draws from a dedicated `adjudication` RNG stream and emits a referee-visibility `DiceRolledEvent`, so an ad-hoc roll is accepted, logged, and replayable without ever perturbing a keyed mechanic's draw sequence.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-07-05

### Added

- The determinism contract: every random draw flows through named PCG64 streams forked from a master seed, so the same seed and the same commands always replay the same game — a public API guarantee.
- Character creation, the seven B/X classes, equipment and encumbrance, and XP-driven leveling, all sourced from the compiled OSE SRD data that ships inside the package.
- The combat kernel: initiative, attacks, damage, saving throws, morale, and death, resolved as pure functions over explicit state.
- Magic: arcane and divine spell books, memorization, casting with disruption, spell effects, and turning undead.
- The crawl: town, travel, and turn-based dungeon exploration — movement, doors, light, listening, searching, traps, rest, and wandering monsters — plus the battle state machine for declared rounds.
- The `GameSession` command/event API: typed commands in, structured events with message codes out, player/referee visibility, views, listeners, and session flags.
- Treasure types A–V, magic items with identification, NPC adventurer parties, and the end-of-adventure XP award.
- Save, load, and replay: stamped JSON documents with schema versioning and forward migrations; a loaded game is bit-for-bit the game you saved.
- Two example front ends — a terminal TUI crawler and a FastAPI HTTP service — proving the engine presentation-agnostic.
- The documentation site: quickstart, guides, front-end walk-throughs, and a full reference for every command, event, rejection code, message code, RNG stream, and content id.
- The typed surface: complete type hints under `py.typed`, checked in CI.
